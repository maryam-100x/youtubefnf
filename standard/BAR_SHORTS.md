# The Shorts bar: what a Short at the level IS

*Part of `STANDARD.md` §1. There are no example videos to watch: this file is the bar in words, detailed enough to build a Short at the
level without ever having seen one. The craft (the method, the tools, the checks): `standard/SHORTS.md`. The rules: `rules/SHORTS.md`.
When the creator signs a Short off as the new standard, it is written in here (what it does at that level, where its files are) and
studied before the next one.*

**Read it as a level, never a template.** What carries from Short to Short is the quality: one reason per picture, move, animation and
sound; the hero dead centre; every line shown, never read; every sound on the thing it shows happening. The devices in the examples
below (a flip, a price tag, a gavel, a stopwatch) were those Shorts' answers to their words. A Short with entirely different elements
can be at exactly this level; a Short that copies these devices onto other words is below it.

## 1. A Short at the bar, in one paragraph
A 30-60 s story about one thing, broken down fast: a first line that puts on the table what viewers hope for or fear and holds back
the payoff to the last line. From frame 0 (a white flash, alone, over a big lit subject that names the story) a new real picture lands
on the word that names it about every 1.4-1.6 s, and every line is acted out on screen: real footage and photos of the thing itself,
real objects cut out and moving like a story, the subject's own logo owning the caption line while its name is said. Nothing on screen
asks to be read but the captions, dead centre, 1-2 words at a time. The hero of every frame sits dead centre and fills the 9:16 frame;
the camera moves only to follow a moving subject or to get from one needed thing to the next. The voice never pauses (every gap ~0.07
s), at about 200 words a minute; its own music sits ~17-18 dB under it, cuts dead on the story's turn and comes back on a downbeat; every
effect lands on the frame of the event it belongs to, and nothing sounds where nothing happens. It ends 0.4 s after the last word.

## 2. The qualities every shot has (with the measured numbers)
The numbers are from the Shorts the creator signed off (23 shots in 36.8 s; 32 in 50.5 s; 30 in 42.1 s) and from the checks that hold
them. "Where" names the check or the place in the build that holds each one.

| Quality | At the bar | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame 0** | The first word is on frame 0. `flash_open` alone (0.25 s of white, no hit layered on it). A subject the eye lands on at once: big (a cut-out ~1.2x the frame's width, a product ~900 px wide, a face in the upper third), lit, moving or pushing. It names the story without being read. Never an empty road, sky or vista, even when it is the exact picture. | proof still of frame 0; the word sheet |
| **Pace** | A new picture every 1.4-1.6 s on average (median 1.34-1.5 s), each landing on the word that names it (a cue is a word in `timing.ts`, never a typed second). No shot past ~2.2 s without a second picture or a big change inside it; no stretch over 2.6 s without a change; the shortest slot ~0.6 s, none under 0.25 s. 3-4 different pictures per fact, none twice inside a fact; a punch-in never counts as a cut. | the shot plan; `qa_holds.py` (the signed-off Shorts' longest stretch: 2.48 s); `measure_edit.py` |
| **Real first** | The thing itself in its best source: the primary source's own footage, press photos, logo and art; official portraits; people's own recordings of the moment; 1080p and up, 4K where it exists, never an AI-upscaled copy. Built only where nothing real shows the line (a count, a mechanism, a time span, a thing no camera filmed), and then built from real cut-outs, real textures and real light, at professional animation quality, never flat shapes. A real object from outside the subject is a fine prop when it acts the line. No stock filler, no AI images. | the shot plan's picture column; `short_visual_check.py` |
| **Shown, never read** | No page, post, headline, statement or card of words is ever a shot's picture; no words are lit. Text that sits inside a real thing is fine (a price on a shelf tag, a sticker on a product, a sign in the street) and may be the point when the viewer will look for that exact text themselves; then a light points at it, the voice says it and the captions carry it. A number lands only as part of a built thing (a tag, a dial, a stopwatch) and only where nothing else shows it. No credits on screen. | `short_visual_check.py` before the gate |
| **Acted out** | Every verb and every named thing is done on screen, on its word: "buy" is a cursor pressing a real button; "refuse" is the slot pushing the cartridge back out; "no" is the object shattering; "two million" is one unit pulling back to a wall of them. A zoom with a sound, a highlight, a crossed-out word are never the act. A small cast of real props carries a story and returns as callbacks (the tease's prop comes back on the payoff). | the shot plan's "why" column |
| **Centred and filling** | The hero dead centre on every frame of a hold and on a pan's last frame, vertically too; a logo on its visual weight (a lettering with a thin left arm can sit 8-9% of its width right of its box centre: measured, gate B21). The important thing big: a cut-out ~1.2x the frame's width with its cut edge off the bottom, a card at w 1000+, never a small picture on a plain ground. A ground behind a piece is real footage, dimmed but at least 0.28 in opacity x brightness, never the piece's own picture blurred. A source under 1080p is a 16:9 card in the upper zone, never blown up. A still is not zoomed past ~2.2x of its pixels (a 4K master allows a deeper zoom). | `frame_review_at.py --cols 6`; gate B4, B21, B22 |
| **The camera** | A hold on a subject that fits: a push of 2-4% a second. A still or a card: ~13% over ~2 s (5% reads as frozen). A pan only with a job: (a) two things the words need that don't fit one window, or (b) a subject that moves, followed key by key the whole slot. A zoom onto a detail opens on the whole picture and lands on its centred subject. Props keep moving after they land (`drift`: 10% a second, at most +18%); nothing still for 0.8 s. A window over a clip whose own camera moves steps with its frames (`lock`). Nothing shakes: a moving card or board is one picture under one transform. | the shot plan's "move" column; `qa_jitter.py`; gate B1 |
| **Zones** | Nothing above y 260; the upper asset at y 290-880; captions dead centre at y ~960; numbers and icons at y 1080-1410; everything inside x 180-900. | `phone_view.py`; the 2-fps sheet |
| **Captions** | Full text, 1:1 with the voice, one size, 1-2 words a chunk, no punctuation but "?", one key phrase a beat in the caption key colour, a name and its number in one caption, every name as the script spells it. A logo or a landing number that says the words owns the caption line and replaces those captions while it shows. | `tools/dj/ass.py`'s planner; `finish_short.py`; gate B10 |
| **Colour** | Everything we draw is white or the caption key colour (a yellow). Colour inside real pictures is theirs. | the build |
| **Light** | A thing pointed at keeps its room readable: the rest dimmed to about a fifth to two fifths of its light, over 0.4 s, the light at least 0.18 x 0.22 of the frame (smaller becomes a glowing disc); a dark shot lifted until it reads (a brightness lift of ~1.2 is common for night footage). | kit3's spot look; gate B22 DARK |
| **Sound** | `flash_open` alone at frame 0. Every effect the house pick for its job (`pal("<job>")`), on the first visible frame of its event, nameable in three words, at most -7 LU under the voice; the same event gets the same sound every time; a light pointing at a thing gets the marker as it starts; one bass drop per Short, on the payoff's key word; no sound on a plain cut or for something the viewer can't see; nothing outlives its picture. Music: the Short's own, ~17-18 dB under the voice in speech, its fuller half entering on a bar where the story opens up, cut dead on the turn and under anyone's own words, back on a downbeat at the next cut. -14 LUFS, ceiling -1.2 dBTP. | `score.py`'s report; the cue sheet |
| **Voice** | About 200 words a minute, the hook at the same speed as the rest; every gap ~0.07 s, sentence ends too (0.1 s tolerance); American from the first word, every section under 0.15 on the accent guard; never a tired or whispered opening. Someone's own words are tightened the same way and covered by a new crisp picture every 2-3 s, the narration running straight into them and out of them. | the sound gate A1-A13; `accent_take.py --voice short` |
| **Story** | One point a line, never restated; everything by its name; every number says what it counts; every person named with their job; a casual viewer has the background within seconds; no time words that date it; the payoff in the last line; it ends 0.4 s after the last word, no end card. | `script_lint.py`; `pipe.py qa` (the end gap) |

**The Short as a whole:** 30-60 s; 23-32 shots; one or two pieces of music with one turn between them; effects only where something
visibly happens (a Short heavy with landings carried 33 in 42 s, a calmer one far fewer: the count follows the events, never a quota); at
most two clips of real people's own words; a few props that return; nothing on screen to read but the captions.

## 3. Three Shorts at the bar, shot by shot
*The facts, names and companies in these examples are invented to show the level (the science one follows the real science); a real
Short sources every fact (`standard/SCRIPTS_AND_PACKAGING.md`). Times are the words' times in the voice. "Built with" names the pieces
as the Shorts kits in `tools/motion/src/shorts/` (`kitS.tsx` and its framing and caption-line pieces), `tools/motion/src/lib.tsx` and
kit3 (`standard/KIT3.md`) export them; "written in the Short's folder" is an act-out no kit piece builds, written for that Short in
`tools/motion/src/<video>/`. Sounds are the house effects by job (`assets/house_sfx.zip`'s manifest; in a cue sheet `pal("<job>")`,
with the marker as `pal("mark")`).*

### 3.1 A product recall: "Check the bottom of your air fryer" (31.8 s, 24 shots, a shot every 1.33 s)

**Script:** [S01] If you've got this air fryer at home, flip it over tonight. [S02] Brisa recalled two million of them, because the
handle can melt and the basket can catch fire. [S03] Over a hundred people reported it overheating, and nine of them got burned.
[S04] Some of them weren't even cooking. It was switched off, on the counter. [S05] It sold everywhere for about eighty dollars, and it
was the best-selling air fryer in the country. [S06] So here's the check. Find the sticker on the bottom, and if the model number
starts with AF2, stop using it. [S07] Brisa will send you a new basket for free, or give you your eighty dollars back.

**Why it works:** the hook is a fear about something in the viewer's own kitchen and an instruction ("flip it over") whose reason and
payoff (what to look for, what you get) wait for the end. The flip at #3 plants the underside the check at #19 returns to; the price
tag of #15 comes back on the carton at #23 and is torn off on "free". **Music:** track A (a tense low pulse) from frame 0, its fuller half entering on a downbeat at
"Brisa recalled"; dead after "counter." (the low point). Track B (steady, lighter) from its own downbeat at "It sold everywhere"; dead
under "stop using it." (a designed stop in `SILENCES`), back on a downbeat at "Brisa will send you" to the end.

### 3.2 A science fact: "The Moon is leaving us" (42.7 s, 28 shots, a shot every 1.53 s)

**Script:** [S01] The Moon is leaving us, and we know exactly how fast. [S02] Every year it drifts about four centimeters farther away,
about as fast as your fingernails grow. [S03] We know because the Apollo astronauts left mirrors up there. [S04] Observatories still
fire lasers at those mirrors and time the light coming back. [S05] That round trip takes about two and a half seconds, and every year
it takes a tiny bit longer. [S06] It's the tides. The Moon pulls the oceans into a bulge, Earth's spin drags that bulge ahead, and the
bulge pulls the Moon forward, out to a wider orbit. [S07] The same pull is slowing Earth down. Corals that grew four hundred million
years ago show four hundred days in a year. [S08] And one day the Moon will look too small to cover the Sun, and there will never be a
total eclipse again.

**Why it works:** the hook opens two questions (how fast, and how do we know) and the end answers the one nobody asked yet (what it
costs us). The pictures are the space agency's own public-domain material wherever a camera saw the thing, and built only for what no
camera can film (the drift, the beam's trip, the tidal bulge), always from the same real cut-outs of Earth and the Moon so the built
shots read as one place. The orbit view returns three times; the stopwatch twice. **Music:** track A (curious, light) from frame 0, its
fuller half on a downbeat at "Observatories"; dead after "wider orbit." (the mechanism is told); track B (deeper) from its own downbeat
at "The same pull" to the end.
