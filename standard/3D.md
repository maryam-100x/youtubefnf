# 3D in our long videos

*Part of `STANDARD.md`, beside `standard/LONG_FORM.md` and `standard/KIT3.md`. Code: `tools/motion/src/kit3d/`. Assets:
`tools/motion/kit3d_assets.py` (into `PUBLIC/kit3d/`). The demo: `src/kit3d/Kit3dDemo.tsx` on drawn material. 3D doesn't change the
editing style; it improves it: one more way to show real material, inside kit3's look (`standard/KIT3.md` §1). Professional-looking 3D
in Remotion, written by Claude, is a real and visible trend among creators; the creator's eye judges it on the first prototype that
uses a piece.*

## 0. In short

1. **What's possible in the stack, with nothing to buy:** real 3D rendered frame by frame in Remotion through `@remotion/three`
   4.0.532 + `@react-three/fiber` 9.3.0 + `three` 0.178 (all in `tools/motion/package.json`). three.js's own examples folder carries
   everything a premium look needs: extruded SVG and font geometry, glTF models, HDR environments, PMREM reflections, MSAA/SSAA, bloom,
   tone mapping (Khronos PBR Neutral), video textures. No `drei`, no `postprocessing` package needed.
2. **What fits the house style:** 3D that makes REAL material more present: the real logo as a lit object (on the day it is the event
   itself), a real screenshot given depth (2.5D parallax), the real Earth at night flying down to the place named, a real clip on a
   phone, a real product's box beside its number. One object, one shaped light, one camera move, and then out.
3. **What doesn't fit:** 3D for its own sake (spinning logos, chrome text, particle storms, neon grids, "tech" wireframes, abstract
   blobs), 3D that replaces a real picture we could show, text set in 3D beyond a single number, anything that looks like a template.
   Every one of them is a tell of the AI-template look (`standard/LONG_FORM.md`: ugly, low effort, the definition of AI slop) and is on
   kit3's never-list.
4. **Built:** five pieces in `kit3d` (`Emblem3D`, `Parallax25D`, `Globe3D`, `Phone3D`, `CaseNumber3D`), each with a demo, stills and a
   clip at 1440p, its render cost measured (section 7) and its licences logged (section 6).

## 1. What fits, and what never does

| Fits (real material, premium, less is more) | Never (the AI-template look in 3D) |
|---|---|
| The real logo extruded from its own SVG, its own artwork on the face, lit like a product (one key, a strip, a rim) | A logo spinning on its axis, its back shown, chrome or neon on it, a lens flare |
| A real screenshot with depth: the subject holds, the world turns behind it, a few % of parallax | Big swoops through a photo (the stretched edges and the filled holes show), depth on a shot with no clear subject |
| The real Earth (NASA's night lights) and a move down to the real place named | A wireframe globe, a "hologram" Earth, arcs of light between cities, stars in the background |
| A real clip or a real page on a phone or screen, turning once to camera | A device with a made-up interface, a branded phone, a floating "app" with icons |
| A real product's box, the number beside it (a number beside its thing, never alone) in the look's display font cut in 3D | A number alone in 3D, rotating digits, 3D text for a whole sentence (text stays HTML, in the look's fonts) |
| One camera move that lands (`EASE.move`), then a slow drift; the shot ends at rest | The camera circling, bobbing ("floating" objects), springing back, moving in the last 0.25 s |
| Bloom only on true highlights (threshold at or above 1; a globe's city lights, 0.82, are light sources themselves), a fine grain, the house vignette | Glow on everything, gradient fog, coloured shadows, glass panels, a second accent colour |
| 3D once or twice a video, where the words are about that object or place | 3D as a style on every card |

kit3's system stays as it is: the look's one ink, the creator's accent only on what the voice names now, the look's fonts only, no
tilted words, graphics once, big, synced, the picture follows the words, no AI-generated or AI-enhanced pictures (depth estimation and
mattes change no pixel; the only made-up pixels are OpenCV's classical inpainting in the slivers a parallax uncovers, section 8 risk 4).

## 3. How 3D renders in Remotion (the rules kit3d keeps, with why)

1. **Every pose is a function of the frame.** Remotion renders frames out of order in several browser tabs, each with its own state.
   `ThreeCanvas` forces `frameloop="never"` during a render and calls `advance()` when the frame changes; R3F's `useFrame` for motion is
   forbidden by Remotion's own 3D skill ("Shaders, models etc MUST NOT animate by themselves"). No clock, no `Math.random`, no simulation
   that integrates over frames, no TAA (it accumulates across frames). Particles, if ever, are a seeded hash of (index, frame).
2. **Load before the canvas mounts.** A texture that arrives after the canvas drew stays black. kit3d loads every file under a
   `delayRender` handle (kit3's `useMeasured`) and mounts the canvas only when all are in; a clip texture calls `advance()` inside
   `onVideoFrame` (Remotion's documented pattern), since `invalidate()` would only schedule a later render.
3. **The camera is set in a layout effect.** `ThreeCanvas`'s frame renderer advances in an effect; a layout effect runs before it, so
   the camera of frame N is in place when frame N is drawn.
4. **The composer renders at priority 1.** A `useFrame` callback with priority over 0 makes R3F skip its own render; that callback is
   only a render, never motion. The composer is built synchronously (a `useMemo` on the renderer), so the first `advance()` already
   outputs.
5. **One canvas per shot.** Each `ThreeCanvas` is a WebGL context; Chrome keeps about 16 per page and drops the oldest after that. Mount
   the 3D shot with its `Sequence`, never premount many.
6. **Text stays HTML.** Labels next to 3D things are kit3's HTML pieces placed with `project(cam, point)` (the same camera math); drei's
   `<Html>` silently drops elements in Remotion renders, and practitioners' own verdict is "keep the diagrams 3D, but make the text 2D".
   The one exception is a number cut from the look's display font itself (`Number3D`).
7. **Colour.** Textures that are pictures are sRGB; the composer renders into a half-float target (HDR), tone-maps once in `OutputPass`
   with Khronos PBR Neutral (made for product renders: base colours stay true under white light, so a logo's gradient and a cover's
   colours don't drift as ACES would push them), then the finish (vignette, grain). A screen or a photo layer is `toneMapped: false` (its
   pixels are already the picture).
8. **Bloom only above 1.** A threshold above 1 in linear light means nothing glows but real specular highlights (react-postprocessing's
   docs: with `luminanceThreshold` 1 "nothing will glow, only the materials you pick"). The one exception is the globe (0.82): its city
   lights are the light sources, and a soft bloom on them is how the night Earth looks.
9. **The gate's probe.** With `REMOTION_GATE=1`, `Stage3D` renders an empty box marked `data-kit3d="<piece>"` and logs it, so the probe
   needs no WebGL. The probe can't see what a 3D shot shows (open risk 2).
10. **The GPU path.** `angle` is the house setting (back to `swangle` for good after a GPU driver reset during a render). Remotion lists
    memory leaks with `angle` as "a known problem" and suggests rendering long videos in parts (`pipe.py render` already renders in
    parts).
11. **Centred by what you see.** A piece that shows ONE object meant to stand on a point never aims at its geometry's origin: `centre.ts`
    projects the piece's own meshes through each frame's camera (three's matrices, no WebGL), rasterises the silhouette, takes the visual
    centre (left to right halfway between the visible box and the centre of mass; up and down the box's middle: `standard/KIT3.md` §5) and
    pans the camera parallel to the picture until it sits on the point (2-3 passes, ~11 ms a frame). The measured meshes are the drawn
    ones (one geometry, shared). Past 2 px, or cut by the frame, the piece refuses; in the gate's probe it logs `kit3d|{frame, box,
    centroid, visual, target}` for `qa_centred.py`'s LOGO_OFF_CENTRE. An emblem aimed at its logo's viewBox centre (= its box centre)
    had its weight 25.6 px right of the point; measured, it lands within 0.2 px on every frame. The same in `Phone3D` (its slab, turned
    as drawn: before, its visual centre sat 84 px left at the cut and 12 at rest; now 0.15) and in `CaseNumber3D`, a GROUP, centred like
    kit3's rows by its whole visible content's box (the case, the standing digits and the HTML under them) on (960, `DS.CY`), on its final
    content from the first frame so nothing slides as the digits stand up (before: 40 px left mid-move, +77 px with an HTML line wider than
    the number; now 0.05). Tested in node: `node src/kit3d/test/centre.test.mjs`.

### 4.2 Premium vs cheap, the documented points

| Point | Source (grade) | In kit3d |
|---|---|---|
| A default area light, "a perfect white rectangle with hard edges", looks fake; use gradients/HDR light maps and "a handful of well-placed lights" | lightmap.co.uk, 5 tips for better studio lighting (B/D) | `STUDIO`: three soft panels baked into the environment, plus one key and one rim |
| "If you're lighting with HDRIs alone, you're doing it wrong": layer sources; avoid flash-like front light | David Ariew, School of Motion (B) | panels + the shot's own picture as the environment + a key + a coloured rim from the ground's light |
| "Reflections are what lights reflective objects"; nothing real is entirely clean | Greyscalegorilla (B) | glossy objects reflect the shot they stand in (`env.photo`) |
| Wrong sRGB texture settings and ACES clipping are what make three.js renders look bad | three.js forum, drcmda and donmccurdy (B/C) | every picture sRGB, one tone map (Neutral) in the composer |
| Bloom restraint: threshold 1 = only chosen highlights glow | react-postprocessing docs (A) | thresholds 0.82 (the globe's city lights) to 1.2 |
| Logo animation: no "territory grab" of the whole frame, no long reveals, nothing floating at the top of an arc | motiondesign.school (B) | the emblem rests at ~50% of the frame height, the move takes 1.8 s, nothing floats |

## 5. The pieces (`tools/motion/src/kit3d/`)

`import {Emblem3D, Parallax25D, Globe3D, Phone3D, CaseNumber3D} from '../kit3d';` Every piece takes the shot's own seconds, mounts its
own canvas, and sits over kit3's `Stage` (the shot's footage defocused) unless it fills the frame itself (Parallax25D, Globe3D). Assets
are public-dir paths built by `kit3d_assets.py` into `PUBLIC/kit3d/`. The props are documented in each file's header.

| Piece (file) | What it is | Use it when the words... | Not when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Emblem3D` (`emblem.tsx`) | A logo made into a lit object: its own artwork on the face (the SVG as a browser draws it, 4096 px), its silhouette extruded with a rounded bevel, the lettering block standing a little prouder, glossy print on the face, dark lacquered sides; it reflects the shot it stands in (`photo`) and a studio of soft panels; a strip of light crosses the face once on the move; a warm rim from behind in the ground's colour; its VISUAL centre on the frame's centre (or `x`, `y`) on every frame (§3 item 11) | name the product or the company as the event itself: the big day lands, "it's finally here", the chapter where the logo IS the subject. **Common props and rules.** Times are the shot's seconds. Each piece makes ONE eased move (`EASE.move`, or `EASE.drift` for the
flight) from the shot's first frame, then only a slow drift (the camera is one move; nothing stands still). Nothing springs back,
nothing moves fast in the last 0.25 s. Credits go through kit3's `Credit` (one line, bottom right). The marker and whoosh sounds come
from the cue sheet as for every graphic: one whoosh on the move's start, the marker on a lit word or a pin. `veil` (default `false`):
see section 8 risk 11.

**How a video uses one** (inside its section's `Sequence`; the 3D canvas over kit3's `Stage` ground; the paths are for illustration):

```tsx
import {Stage, Credit} from '../kit3';
import {Emblem3D, Parallax25D, Globe3D, Phone3D, CaseNumber3D} from '../kit3d';
// the logo as the event, over the shot's own footage
<Stage dur={d} ground="<video>/clips/s03_hall_night.mp4" />
<Emblem3D shape="kit3d/logos/<logo>_shape.json" dur={d} photo="<video>/img/s03_hall_night_still.jpg" rim="#ffc58a" />
<Credit pic="The maker's official logo" />
// a screenshot with depth (built once: kit3d_assets.py parallax --photo <name>=<picture>)
<Parallax25D src="kit3d/img/<name>" dur={d} />
// a number beside its box
<CaseNumber3D dur={d} cover="kit3d/img/<cover>.jpg" value="45" unit="million units" subject="sold in its first year"
  at={w('S12', 'forty') - t0} lit={w('S12', 'five') - t0} />
```

**A new logo, screenshot, product or clip:** `EDIT tools/motion/kit3d_assets.py emblem --logo <file.svg>` (traces any SVG into its face
and outline), `parallax --photo <name>=<picture>` (the four layers of a screenshot), `basics --cover <picture>`, `clip --clip <video> [--ss
0] [--t 5]`, `earth` (the world map and the detail patch). The 3D pieces read only public-dir paths.

## 7. The proof (run it after any change to kit3d)

Runnable again, from `tools/motion`:

1. **Assets** (no Remotion job): `EDIT tools/motion/kit3d_assets.py all` builds `PUBLIC/kit3d/` and the demo's lean public dir
   `TMP/pub_kit3d` (hard links). The licensed files placed by hand (the HDRI, the fonts, the NASA world map: `--world <file>`) are listed
   by `basics` with their sources.
2. **Stills, clips and timing** (one Remotion job, the heavy ticket): `bash with_remotion_lock.sh "kit3d proof" node kit3d_proof.mjs
   MEDIA/videos/kit3d/<run> all` (one lean bundle, one Chrome, GL `angle`; stills at 2560 x 1440 = `--scale 4/3` as `pipe.py` renders;
   each demo rendered twice: at concurrency 1 for the true cost of a frame, then at the house concurrency 6, kept as the clip, H.264 CRF
   16).
3. **Determinism** (no Remotion job): `PIPE tools/motion/kit3d_check.py MEDIA/videos/kit3d/<run>` compares every still (one tab,
   rendered alone) with the same frame of the clip (six tabs in parallel, each its own WebGL context, asset loads and frame order).

| Case and number (`CaseNumber3D`) | 300 | 0.110 | 0.066 | 1.0-1.2 s |

So a 3D shot costs about 0.07-0.13 s a frame at the house concurrency: a 5-s 3D shot adds 20-40 s to a render (a plain long-video
frame was 41 ms on the GPU). Six tabs gain only ~1.5x over one (0.084 against 0.125 for the emblem); a reading, not measured: the one
GPU and the frame capture are the limit, not the CPU. The phone gains least (its clip is also decoded for the texture in every tab).

**Determinism: 22 of 22 frames the same.** Every still (one tab, alone) against the same frame of its clip (six tabs): PSNR 36.3-41.7
dB against a codec floor of 37.7-42.3 dB (the still sent through the clip's own JPEG q92 + x264 CRF 16 path), the worst 8 x 8 block at
most 5.4 above the floor's worst. The check's first run caught a real fault: a globe pin was accent-coloured in the still and ink in the
clip (63 against a floor of 12 at the pin), because a mutated `THREE.Color` passed as a prop is never re-applied by R3F (same reference):
a tab that first drew the pin unlit kept it unlit. Fixed (the piece owns the material and sets its colour each render), re-rendered, the
same.

## 8. Open risks

1. **The creator's judgement is still to come.** Everything here is measured against the house rules and the research, not against the
   creator's eye. The first real test is the first prototype that uses a piece; their verdict goes into `cases/PICTURE.md`.
2. **The gate can't see inside a 3D shot.** Its probe gets an empty `data-kit3d` box (no WebGL in the probe): no B-checks on what the
   canvas shows, no clip-source check for a clip on a phone screen (B0 reads clips only through `lib.tsx`'s `Clip`). The HTML around it
   (Tag, Credit, unit and subject) is checked as usual. `Emblem3D`, `Phone3D` and `CaseNumber3D` measure themselves (§3 item 11) and log
   `kit3d|` lines that `qa_centred.py` reads; an incremental probe (`reprobe.py`) must keep reading those lines (its frame pattern must
   include `kit3d` beside `layout` and `refusal`); each piece's own refusal (B15) holds meanwhile.
3. **Clips are full frame, and the phone.** A clip on a phone screen is footage inside a device. It's a device shown (the words are about
   phones), not a panel shrinking a clip, but a viewer may read it as one: use it only where the phone is the point, and let the creator
   judge it in the prototype.
4. **Parallax fill.** The background behind the subject is filled by OpenCV's Telea inpainting (classical, no generated content). It's
   a smear: at full resolution a soft, darker band shows beside a head when the move uncovers it (visible at 1440p with a 0.16 slide).
   Keep the default slide (0.14) or smaller; a better classical fill (exemplar or FSR inpainting from opencv-contrib) is the next step if
   the creator likes the piece. AI fill (LaMa) only on the creator's word.
5. **Depth model limits.** Depth-Anything-V2-Small is relative depth from one picture: fine for people before a city, unreliable for
   glass, water, reflections and thin structures (railings, branches), which can warp. Pick screenshots with a clear subject.
6. **The globe's detail stops at the patch.** The world map is 3 km a pixel; only the detail patch (the bounds `globe.tsx`'s `PATCH`
   names) has ~1 km. A low view anywhere else blurs: set `PATCH` to the place and run `kit3d_assets.py earth` first (it crops NASA's
   500-m tile B1, 90W-0 and 0-90N; a place outside it needs its own tile, a ~30-MB file per 90 x 90 degrees, decoded at half size to stay
   under ~350 MB of RAM).
7. **Trademark.** An extruded logo is its owner's mark used to name the subject (referential, commentary). Its lettering is the
   "distinctive lettering" New Kids' footnote warns about; YouTube handles trademark complaints by uploader contact first. Never use it as
   the channel's branding, on merch, or in a sponsor segment.
8. **The GPU.** Every 3D frame is drawn on the GPU that the creator's other GPU work (a stream, a recording) may also use; a driver reset
   during a render sends the house setting to `swangle`, and kit3d on `swangle` (software) is **untested**. Remotion warns that `angle`
   leaks memory on long renders: `pipe.py`'s parts handle that.
9. **Bundle size.** kit3d pulls `three/examples` loaders and passes into the bundle only through `src/kit3d/`; `Root.tsx` doesn't import
   kit3d. A video that uses it imports it in its own body file.
10. **Not built (yet):** a device or product from a licensed model (Sketchfab CC-BY downloads need the creator's account; `GLTFLoader` is
    in three already, and `Case3D`'s lighting would carry over), a 3D map (a map on a tilted plane with real terrain is possible; a map
    someone else made keeps its maker's credit), particles (none fit the house look so far).
11. **The bloom veils the ground under a transparent canvas: `veil` is `false` by default.** three's `UnrealBloomPass` writes alpha 1 from
    its blur and adds it at the bloom's strength (0.2 x its five mips' factors 3.0 = 0.6): the canvas was black at alpha 153/255 wherever
    nothing was drawn, so kit3's `Stage` under an `Emblem3D` (and `Phone3D` 0.45, `CaseNumber3D` 0.36) showed at 0.4 of its `dim`
    (measured: a dark interior's ground at 0.39-0.42 of the Stage alone; mean 20.5 veiled against 49.9 at its own dim, 2.44x). Shown side by
    side, the brighter one was picked: `veil` (core.tsx `PostSpec.veil`, a prop on the three pieces) defaults to `false`: the bloom's colour
    blends as before, the canvas's alpha is left as the scene drew it, the ground shows at its own `dim`. `veil={true}` brings the darker
    look back, only to match a shot the creator passed with it. Parallax25D and Globe3D draw on an opaque canvas: the veil never touched
    them.
