# Shorts editing: every element with a job

*The aim: a Short that is more creative, more visual, more appealing and more calculated, where every cut, zoom, pan, pop-in,
animation and sound has a written purpose. The house rules for this live in `rules/SHORTS.md` and `standard/SHORTS.md`; this file is the
evidence behind them. A worked example of the idea: the Short opens on a company's logo alone on a plain ground; on the word "lawyers",
three lawyers' cut-outs rise from the bottom; no captions while that plays. The camera rule (`rules/SHORTS.md`) is that a pan has only
two jobs, (a) moving from one target to the next when both don't fit 9:16, or (b) following a subject that moves; otherwise the picture
holds, with a slight push at most. The reason is in §2: a frame that moves without a job competes with the subject for the eye.*

*Read with: `standard/research/SHORTS_CRAFT.md` (metrics, hooks, pace, captions, loops), `standard/SHORTS.md` (the bar),
`standard/SOUND_AND_VOICE.md` and `standard/research/MOTION_CRAFT.md`. This file adds: the official 9:16 safe-zone numbers, the 2025-2026
platform changes, and the lab science behind "acting out" devices (multimedia learning, attention capture, habituation), turned into
build rules.*

**Grades.** **A** = primary or measured (platform docs, peer-reviewed studies, our own pixel measurement). **B** = credible practitioner or
vendor with a stated sample or shown examples. **C** = opinion, listicle, or a figure seen only in a search summary. Dates are publication
dates.

**Honest headline.** The 2025-2026 web is flooded with tool-vendor SEO pages that repeat each other ("a visual change every 2-3 s",
"captions +25%") with no method. Text sources from editors who show their edits are rare; most of that lives in YouTube videos, which an
agent can't transcribe (YouTube's bot check). So the strongest evidence is (1) platform specs and policy, (2) laboratory research on how
viewers attend to pictures, words and sounds, and (3) our own measurements of a batch of top Shorts we studied. Where an outside "rule"
has no data behind it, this file says so.

## 0. What to do in the next Short (18 build rules)

| # | Rule (buildable) | Why | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Every element gets a job line in the shot plan before it's built**: the word it lands on, what it shows, why. No job, no element. | Removing interesting-but-irrelevant material is one of the most reliable gains in multimedia research: coherence principle, median d = 0.86 (23 of 23 tests). Seductive details lowered learning across 58 studies, with a considerable effect in lessons **under 5 minutes** (none significant over 10) | A |
| 2 | **Nothing decorative stays on screen**: no persistent badge, frame, sticker or emoji layer. | Seductive details hurt most "when it was constant, such as a static joke or image on a screen" and when placed next to the relevant picture (Sundararajan & Adesope 2020) | A |
| 3 | **The named thing appears on its word**: its first frame within ~2 frames of the word's onset, settled by ~200 ms. | Temporal contiguity (picture and narration at the same time): median d = 1.30, the largest of Mayer's design effects. | 4 | **Entrances loom or rise toward the viewer; exits recede or fade.** A cut-out grows from ~0.85x to 1.0x with a small overshoot, or rises from the bottom edge (the lawyers example); leaving elements shrink or fade fast. | Looming and moving onsets capture attention automatically; receding objects did not (Franconeri & Simons 2003; contested only for stereo depth). The arriving thing takes the eye, the leaving thing doesn't fight it | A |

## 1. Acting out the narration: what the evidence supports

### 1.1 The science that applies (multimedia learning, read as abstracts and summaries)
- **Temporal contiguity, d = 1.30; multimedia (words + pictures vs words alone), d = 1.67; segmenting d = 0.70; modality d = 0.72**
  (Mayer 2017, *J. Computer Assisted Learning* 33(5), doi 10.1111/jcal.12197; values from the abstract listing on metatoc). **A**
- **Coherence d = 0.86 (23 of 23 tests); signaling d = 0.41 (24 of 28)** (Mayer, *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*, chapter on
  reducing extraneous processing). A later review lists coherence 0.70, signaling 0.46, redundancy 0.87 (search summary, not read). **A**
- **Verbal redundancy** (Adesope & Nesbit 2012, *J. Educ. Psych.* 104(1):250-263, 57 studies): spoken + written beat spoken-only, the
  advantage appearing for low prior knowledge, **system-paced** material (video is) and **picture-free** material; key terms instead of
  verbatim text accounted for much of it. **A**. For the build: captions help over plain talk; a picture that carries the line wants the
  screen to itself, or one key word.
- **Seductive details** (Sundararajan & Adesope 2020, *Educ. Psych. Review* 32:707-734; 58 studies, 7,500+ students): learners with
  them scored lower; worse when constant and when next to relevant diagrams ([WSU, 2020-03-19](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning));
  considerable under 5 minutes, n.s. over 10 ([Learning and the Brain, 2020-04-25](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/seductive-details-when-do-cool-stories-and-videos-interfere-with-learning/)).
  A g ≈ -0.33 circulates; unverified. **A via B summaries**
- **Limits, stated plainly:** these measure learning (recall, transfer), not watch time or swipes. They tell us what the viewer *takes
  in*; that a Short people understand is a Short people finish is our reasoning, not a measured fact.

### 1.2 Attention capture: why entrances matter
- **Looming and translating objects capture attention; receding ones did not** (Franconeri & Simons 2003, *Perception & Psychophysics*
  65(7):999-1010). Abrams & Christ (2005) argue motion onset itself is the trigger; von Mühlenen & Lleras (2007) found capture for looming
  only once onsets were controlled. **A**. Build: the thing the word names enters by growing toward the viewer or rising; whatever must
  not steal the eye leaves by shrinking or fading, never by flying across.
- **Every onset is a bid for the eye.** So an element without a job (§0 item 1) actively pulls attention off the one with a job. This
  is the mechanism behind "every element has a purpose".

### 1.3 Device catalogue (when each has a purpose)
| Device | Its job (use it when...) | Build notes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut-out entering on its word** (people, products, logos) | The line names a person/thing/group the footage can't show | Rise from the bottom edge or loom 0.85→1.0 with a 3-5% overshoot, settled in 150-250 ms; ~1.2x frame width, cut edge off the bottom (`standard/SHORTS.md`); a soft contact shadow | 1.2; temporal contiguity A; our own reviews (OURS) |
| **Group of N** (three lawyers, five days) | The number is the point | Staggered 4-8 frames apart, same sound on each (§0 item 14); the count said in the voice, not shown as a card (`rules/SHORTS.md`) | Signaling A; `standard/research/MOTION_CRAFT.md` §2 offsets (C) |
| **Prop animation** (a padlock shuts, a stamp lands, a counter runs) | A verb in the line can be shown happening | The action completes on the verb's stressed syllable; the sound sits on the completion frame | Temporal contiguity A; a padlock that shut on its verb passed our review (OURS) |
| **Big designed word** (kinetic type) | One word is the hook or the payoff and no picture says it better | Replaces the caption for that word (text shows once: `rules/`); heavy condensed face; 1-2 words | Adesope key terms A |
| **Receipt** (page with marked words) | A claim needs proof on screen | 2-6 words marked as said; the narrator paraphrases around the quote | Signaling A; YPP A |
| **Split screen** | Two things must be compared at the same moment | Both halves filled, meeting in the middle (a lesson from our own reviews); never for one subject | Spatial contiguity (median d ~0.8, Mayer) A; the batch we studied (OURS) |
| **Before/after reveal** | The change itself is the story | One wipe or slide, motion-blurred, on the word that names the change | No measured study found |
| **Zoom onto a detail** (punch-in or inset) | The word names a small thing in a wide frame | Instant punch on the word, or `standard/SHORTS.md`'s `inset` | Lang 2000 A; the top-tier Short we studied (OURS) |
| **Icon/emoji pop** | Almost never: only when an icon is the real thing (a real app's interface icon, an app logo) | Real asset, not a generic glyph (`rules/`) | Seductive details A; our own reviews judged generic glyphs cheap (OURS) |

### 1.4 What reads cheap or overdone (2025-2026)
- **Decoration that stays** (persistent stickers, frames, emoji): the worst case in the seductive-details meta-analysis (A).
- **Every word animated with its own effect; overlapping text**: named in several kinetic-type guides as the commonest mistake (C).
- **One house look cloned across thousands of channels** ("Hormozi-style" captions, constant zooms): editors call it saturated; no data
  (C). The vendor view for 2026 is that "generic short-form" stopped working and a recognisable frame language wins ([OpusClip,
  2026-05-12](https://www.opus.pro/blog/editing-aesthetics-dominating-short-form-2026), C).
- **Templates and slideshows**: a measured cost, not taste: YPP demonetizes them (A, §8).

## 2. The camera in a 9:16 reframe

- **The two-jobs pan rule is backed by the eye-tracking we have.** A subject that moved around the 9:16 frame produced more saccades and
  shorter fixations than one held centrally (Baig et al. 2025, 40 viewers on their own phones, summarized by [RealEye, 2025-11-03](https://www.realeye.io/blog/post/the-gravity-of-the-center-what-grabs-attention-in-vertical-videos); no numbers published there). **B**
- **Following a moving hero is the right pan.** In free viewing of dynamic scenes, gaze clusters around motion, and a single moving
  person gave the strongest agreement between viewers (Smith & Mital 2013, *J. Vision*; Mital et al. 2011, *Cognitive Computation*;
  abstracts). A window that keeps that person centred keeps every viewer's eyes in the same place. **A**
- **An unmotivated pan moves the whole frame**, which is global motion competing with the subject for the eye (our reasoning from the
  above). That is why "panning for the sake of it" reads as noise.
- **Pushes.** Our measured floor: a 5% push read as frozen; receipts pushing 13% over 1.9 s read alive (`standard/SHORTS.md`). "A slight
  push at most" needs a number between those; see §9.
- **Punch-in vs push.** A punch-in is an instant re-framing onto a named detail (a related within-scene edit, Lang 2000); a push is a
  slow drift that keeps a hold alive. Different jobs, both allowed.
- **Whips and speed ramps:** no measured evidence found. The batch of top Shorts we studied uses whip-blurs only at section turns (two in
  28 s in the top-tier one).
- **Practitioner reframing advice** (vendors, C): keyframe the window only where the subject moves; auto-reframe falls back to a centre
  crop when there's no face. Nothing beyond what `tools/motion/frame_review_at.py` already does.

## 3. Pacing and rhythm

- **Relatedness sets the speed** (Lang's line of work, A): related cuts raise arousal and memory without overload; unrelated cuts cost
  capacity; fast pace with arousing content overloads. §0 item 11 follows from it.
- **Camera changes don't wear out; production effects can.** Camera changes kept eliciting orienting responses after many repetitions
  (Lang 1990; Lee & Lang 2015, as reviewed in [Potter et al. 2018, *J. of Cognition* 1(1):39](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6634355/)). **A**
- **2026's attention data:** average view duration per Short fell ~67% to ~16 s between Feb 2025 and Feb 2026 while Shorts views rose
  127% ([Metricool, 2026-07-29](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/), 799,718 videos from 71,177 accounts; no length breakdown). **B**
  More Shorts are started and fewer are watched far, so the first third carries the Short.
- **YouTube added 2x playback for Shorts** ([YouTube blog, 2026-06-25](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-shorts-experience-updates-features/)). **A** Viewers who want speed
  can now take it; a calm beat no longer has to be cut away for them.
- **Vendor cadence rules** ("re-hook every 3-5 s", 1of10 2025-04-14; "every 1.5-2 s", various 2026): no data behind any of them (C). Our
  measurements of the batch of top Shorts we studied remain the measured cadence.

## 4. The first 1-3 seconds and the end

- **Frame 0 is looked at in the middle first.** At a clip's onset, viewers' gaze starts in a central cluster (Smith & Henderson; Tseng et
  al. 2009, *J. Vision*, who attribute much centre bias to setup and photographer bias). **A** So frame 0's subject goes dead centre on a
  quiet ground; anything else on frame 0 is competition.
- **One idea across picture, voice and type** (§0 item 8). Google's Shorts ABCDs (ads): "Be loud and clear", "Announce yourself with
  audio", "Use tight framing", "Emphasize the product with a see & say approach" ([Google one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf), undated, read via pdftotext). **A for ads**
- **First-frame text** as a separate hook (the vendor consensus, and Kallaway's "triple hook") conflicts with the house rules
  (`rules/SHORTS.md`): the house puts the hero word in the caption line (`standard/research/SHORTS_CRAFT.md` §2.4). Nothing new
  overturns that.
- **Ads only, unread pages:** TikTok's Creative Center reports text at 5-10 words a second as its "sweet spot" (+2.1x awareness); a
  VidMob/TikTok analysis of 1,678 ads found a talking head lifted 2-second view rate 14% (both seen in search summaries; pages 500/404). **C**
- **The end and the loop:** nothing new beyond `standard/research/SHORTS_CRAFT.md` §6. In the logo example, ending on the same plain
  ground the Short opened on makes the loop to the logo seamless; vidIQ's loop guide adds "check that any audio crosses the restart
  cleanly" (C).

## 5. Sound design (adds to `standard/SOUND_AND_VOICE.md` only)

- **Orienting to sound features** ([Potter et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6634355/), A, with the studies it reviews):
  - Voice changes elicit orienting and did not habituate after six occurrences (Potter 2000).
  - Sound effects, production effects and jingles produce the orienting response (Potter, Lang & Bolls 2008).
  - In passive listening, production effects habituated **by the third onset** (Potter, Lynch & Kraus 2015).
  - Listeners busy on their phones (texting, browsing: the way Shorts are watched) showed **no habituation** over five repeats (2018,
    n = 27).
  - Read: a repeated sound isn't proven to wear out on phone viewers, but the safe build is §0 item 14: one-off reveals get their own
    sound.
- **The voice change is the strongest sound cue.** An original clip's own voice entering (original clips play with their own sound:
  `rules/`) re-orients the viewer every time. Our reasoning from the above, not a measured Shorts effect.
- **Sound-on:** YouTube said 96% of its audience watches with sound on (2016, pre-Shorts, [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/youtube-takes-a-shot-at-facebook-our-audience-continues-to-watch-not-scroll-with-sound-on/)). The June 2026 player
  added a mute button behind a tap-to-pause. **No Shorts-specific sound-on figure found.**
- **Levels, risers, silence:** no new measured source beyond `standard/SOUND_AND_VOICE.md` (searched; only library pages and glossaries).

## 6. Captions and on-screen text in 2026

- **When to drop captions for a picture or a designed word:** Adesope & Nesbit 2012 (§1.1), A. Captions earn their place over talk on
  ambient footage; a designed element that carries the line wants the screen alone (the lawyers example), or one key word as type.
- **Where captions sit:** a student eye-tracking thesis (Utrecht) found no comprehension difference between caption designs and far fewer
  fixations on captions at the top than in the middle (summary only; page 403). **C**. The house's dead-centre captions stand.
- **Clear Screen mode** (YouTube blog, 2026-06-25): one tap hides every icon and all text overlays of the player (A). Most viewers still
  see the default overlays; the safe zones below still apply.
- **Heart replaces the like; the dislike is retired**; "Not interested" and "Don't recommend this channel" become the negative signals
  (same post, A).

## 8. Policy: the measured cost for a narrated, AI-voiced Short

[YouTube Help, YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) (the "repetitious content" policy was renamed
"inauthentic content" on 2025-07-15). **A**
- Not monetizable: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative"; "AI-generated content made
  with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production".
- Monetizable: "the same intro and outro ... but the bulk of your content is different"; each video with "a distinct storyline, focus, or
  concept".
- Reused content, not monetizable: "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create".
- Reused content, monetizable: "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary"; "Edited footage with audio
  and visual effects on top".
- **For a narrated Short:** designed scenes that act out the channel's own script are the strongest proof of an original storyline. A
  receipt read aloud sentence by sentence is the riskiest thing a narrated Short does.

## 9. Points to check against the house files

1. **Captions "full text, 1:1 with the voice"** vs the logo and lawyers example, the house rule that narration needn't be fully captioned
   (`rules/`) and Adesope & Nesbit (A): a caption rule should say captions step aside while a designed element carries the line.
2. **"Nothing stands still ... a 5% push read as frozen" (13% reads alive) in `standard/SHORTS.md`** vs the pan rule's "a slight push at
   most": no contradiction on pans, but "slight" needs a number the creator signs off (a side-by-side of ~6%, 9%, 13% over 2 s on one
   still would settle it).
3. **House zones vs Google's vertical overlay:** right edge 900 vs 887; the numbers/icons band reaches y 1410 vs the ad line at 1246;
   receipts drawn 940 wide reach past both. Probably fine for organic Shorts; unverified.
4. **The house rule of no shot past ~2.2 s without a cut, cut-away or punch-in (`rules/SHORTS.md`)** vs the batch of top Shorts we
   studied: 6 of 9 have a stretch of 4 s+ with no hard cut, each carried by change inside the shot. Not a clash if pop-ins and in-shot
   builds count as the change (`tools/motion/qa_holds.py` already counts "a change"); worth writing down.
5. **"Items that appear one by one each get the same sound" (`standard/SHORTS.md`)** vs habituation research: mixed evidence (passive
   listeners habituate by the 3rd repeat; phone-busy listeners didn't). Keep it for sets; give one-off reveals their own sound.
6. **Outside practice: a separate text hook on frame 0** (Kallaway, vendors) vs the house rules (`rules/SHORTS.md`): already settled for
   the hero word in the caption line (`standard/research/SHORTS_CRAFT.md` §2.4); repeated here only because 2026 guides still push it.
