# Shorts craft: how the best Shorts are edited for retention

*The question: how do the best Shorts editors (2025-2026) edit for "viewed vs swiped away", average percentage viewed and loops, and
how does Shorts editing differ from long form. This file is the evidence; the house rules it touches live in standard/SHORTS.md and
rules/SHORTS.md, and where a house rule and outside advice differ, the house rule wins.*

## 1. How the Shorts system and its metrics work now

### 1.1 How a Short finds viewers
- **The feed pulls per viewer; it does not push a video.** "It isn't so much about pushing it out as much as it's pulling ... for each
  viewer" (Todd Beaupré, YouTube growth and discovery lead, [Creator Insider, 2025-01-23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dhYIb72L1hU)). **[YT]**
- **A new Short is tried on a seed audience, then grows or tapers.** "Those algorithms will go and effectively find like a seed audience
  ... and depending on how that goes, it may get a lot more traffic or it may kind of taper off" (Todd Sherman, Shorts product lead,
  [Creator Insider, 2023-08-23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU)). In 2025 he calls it "exploration through the algorithm"
  with "test audience[s]" ([Creator Insider with Jordan Howlett, 2025-07-31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LsJD6qPBP5o)). **[YT]**
- **Things that do not matter, per YouTube (2023-08-23, same video):** time of day ("No"), hashtags (not required, can help for events),
  a daily upload count ("nothing in our systems that's like when you hit the five video or 10 short threshold"). Deleting and re-posting
  a Short that flopped: "I would not advise that ... there is a risk there that that gets seen as spam in our systems." **[YT]**
- **Freshness does matter for news:** "if somebody's focused on news, then the timeliness of that could matter" (2023-08-23); "when you serve
  fresher videos ... they see things that are more current" (2025-07-31). **[YT]** How a timely Short is worded so it still works later:
  the house standard sets this (rules/SHORTS.md).
- **Low-effort crops lose.** "The ones that tend to get the best results are not just cropping part of their long form video ... they're
  putting a bit more effort into it" (Todd Sherman, 2025-07-31). Unedited clips of others' films or shows, and "compilations with no
  original content added", are not eligible for Shorts revenue ([YouTube for Creators, Shorts page](https://www.youtube.com/creators/shorts/)). **[YT]**
- **Formats are matched per viewer inside one channel.** "If we can see that a particular viewer ... tend[s] to only consume the short
  content versus the long content, then we will" match that (Todd Beaupré, [Creator Insider, 2026-09-01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY)). **[YT]**

### 1.2 The metrics (what Studio shows, what each one answers)
| Metric | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shown in feed** | How often the Short was put in the Shorts feed (the Shorts version of impressions) | [1of10, 2026-09-12](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/) **[DATA]**, label in Studio |
| **Viewed vs swiped away** ("How many chose to view", "Stayed to watch") | Share of feed appearances where the viewer stayed instead of swiping. "Not the same as click-through rate, but it's a way you can get an idea of how many people are viewing your shorts" | Todd Sherman, [2023-08-23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU) **[YT]** |
| **Views** | Since **2025-03-31**: "the number of times your short starts to play or replay with no minimum watch time" | [Creator Insider, 2025-03-26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-QM6O4yPfrI); [TechCrunch, 2025-03-26](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted) **[YT]** |
| **Engaged views** | The old view: "how many viewers chose to continue watching". In Advanced Mode. Earnings and YPP run on it (3M valid public Shorts views in 90 days for early access, 10M for ad revenue). Compare pre-2025-03-31 Shorts with engaged views only | Same Creator Insider video **[YT]** |
| **Average view duration / average % viewed** | Can pass 100% when viewers loop | Jenny Hoyos ([Creator Science, 2023-10](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)) **[PRACT]**; a looped Short of ours at 102.7% **[OURS]** |

- **The threshold behind a "view" is secret on purpose:** YouTube does not publish it because it may tweak it for every new surface, and it
  does not want people optimizing against it (Todd Sherman, 2023-08-23). **[YT]**
- One guide quotes Studio's tooltip that an engaged view is a viewer who "stayed to watch past the initial seconds, not including any loops"
  ([adaptlypost](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/youtube-engaged-views)). **Unverified** against a YouTube page. **[VENDOR]**
- Why the 2025 change: on long form a view should be "approximately a tap or a click"; in Shorts "people are not selecting a specific
  video. They're swiping in a feed ... This is like an unselection" (Todd Sherman and Rene Ritchie, 2025-07-31). **[YT]**

### 1.3 What the system rewards (as far as it is public)
- **No single number.** "There's no single answer ... different factors can be ... of different importance in different contexts"; YouTube
  adds "satisfaction" signals: in-product surveys ("millions of responses"), likes, dislikes, "not interested" (Todd Beaupré, 2025-01-23). **[YT]**
- **Compare to yourself.** Absolute CTR or watch time is "not really comparable like across channels" (2025-01-23). **[YT]** Studio's
  "compared to your typical performance" is the benchmark ([bytecap, 2026-07-17](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)). **[DATA]**
- **Paddy Galloway's summary of his 5,400-Short study:** "YouTube will personalize recommendations of Shorts ... and show enjoyable Shorts
  that hold attention well to them. Simple." (his X thread, reported by [nowbam, 2023-04-25](https://nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). **[PRACT]**
- **Retention is not everything (the strongest practitioner counterpoint).** Jenny Hoyos (~10M views per Short): "I've had a short ...
  with 70% retention on the first 100k views ... and it still got 10,000,000 views because it was all returning viewers"; her hunches are
  shares and viewer satisfaction ([Creator Science, 2023-10](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)). **[PRACT]**
- **Loops/rewatches as a ranking signal: no YouTube statement found.** Replays count as views (fact, 2025-03-31); that rewatch rate is
  weighted in ranking is a vendor claim everywhere it appears. **Unconfirmed.**

### 1.4 Benchmarks, and where a young channel stands
| | Viewed vs swiped away (stayed) | Average % viewed |
|---|---|---|
| Paddy Galloway, 33 channels / 5,400 Shorts (2023) | "keep it north of 60%", but don't obsess **[PRACT]** | AVD > 50 s averaged 4.1M views vs 1.8M at 40-50 s **[DATA]** |
| Jenny Hoyos (2023-10) | "average ... is like 70%"; hers 85% **[PRACT]** | Hers 95%; "90 plus percent ... in my opinion, the bare minimum" for a blow-up; under 30 s she needs > 100% **[PRACT]** |
| 1of10 (2026-09-12), community-reported, "not official" | 70%+ workable, 80%+ strong **[DATA]** | Longer Shorts post lower % naturally **[DATA]** |
| quso.ai, 508K posts / 108K matured Shorts (updated 2026-06-29) | n/a | Median ~45%; bottom 90% ~40%; top 10% ~91% ([quso](https://quso.ai/research/video-retention-benchmarks)) **[DATA]**, a clip-tool's users, mostly small creators |
| **Our own first 17 Shorts** | **32.3-69.5%, average 56.4%** | **55-103%, average 68.7%** **[OURS]** |

**Reasoning (ours):** every one of those 17 Shorts landed between 1.0K and 2.8K views whatever its stayed rate, i.e. none got past its seed
audience; shares were about 0.03% of views. The stayed rate sat below every external mark (60/70/80%). The swipe decision, the first
1-2 seconds, is the bottleneck before any later editing can matter. That is where the editing effort pays most.

## 2. The first 1-3 seconds: hooks

### 2.1 What the sources say
- **YouTube:** "Whether you're making a 15-second video or a 60-second video, grabbing people in the first few seconds tends to be important"
  (Todd Sherman, 2023-08-23). **[YT]**
- **The first frame is the thumbnail.** "Most thumbnails are never seen because people are in the feed and they're swiping" (Todd Sherman,
  2023-08-23) **[YT]**. "Treat your video like a thumbnail" (Paddy Galloway, 2023) **[PRACT]**. "In Shorts, frame one is the thumbnail, the hook,
  and the promise all at once"; the stay-or-swipe decision falls "in roughly the first 1 to 2 seconds" (1of10, 2026-09-12). **[DATA/opinion]**
- **Jenny Hoyos's hook rules** ([Creator Science, 2023-10](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)) **[PRACT]**:
  - "If it could be used as a title and thumbnail on a long form and ... still get clicks, then it'll work for a short."
  - "The hook needs to be very visual. You need to understand it without even listening to it."
  - She checks the hook's wording with a readability checker: "5th grade or under".
  - Structure: **hook, then a two-line foreshadow of the ending, "usually 3 seconds or less"**, then a transition that does not break pace
    ("instead of saying, let's get started, I said, so I cooked illegally").
  - The middle runs on "but / therefore" changes, not "and then".
- **A second-by-second teardown of her 21M-view Short** ([Demand Curve #187, 2024-06-11](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-187)) **[PRACT]**:
  "Get right into it. No pre-amble"; "Don't waste time saying anything explained visually"; re-hook mid-video by restating the premise.
- **Context plus curiosity gap** (heyDominik, "I Studied 1,000 Hooks", [2025-07-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xqy9L-88wcw), 813K views) **[PRACT]**:
  viewers swipe "on autopilot" until something makes the brain go "hm"; a hook without context loses them; "challenges a common belief"
  is "one of the strongest forms"; the text hook must sit inside the safe zone and is usually "way smaller than you might think".
- **Unsourced number:** "50-60% of viewers who drop off do so within the first three seconds" ([OpusClip blog, 2025-11-11](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)).
  The same page still says Shorts cap at 60 s. **[VENDOR]**, do not use.

### 2.2 What our reference batches measured **[OURS]**
- Batch 2 (5 top Shorts we studied): **all five start speaking at 0.00 s; the first visual payoff comes by 2.4 s.**
- Batch 3 (9 top Shorts we studied): **first cut at 1.1 s median (0.4-3.2 s)**; our own Shorts at the time were 0.3-2.75 s.
- Batch 1 (2 top Shorts we studied): first word at 0.00 s in both; no pause over 0.14 s in one of them.

### 2.3 The house hook rules
What the first words must do, which names lead, what frame 0 shows and sounds like, when the voice starts and at what speed the hook is
spoken: the house standard sets these (standard/SHORTS.md, rules/SHORTS.md). One observed failure behind them: a Short whose first
6 seconds held the same clip the whole time read as the worst-edited part; a new picture after the first hero word fixed it.

### 2.4 Hook types worth building (each tied to a source)
| Type | What it looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Provocative claim / against a belief | "The year's most hyped film WON'T win Best Picture" | heyDominik **[PRACT]** |
| Known name first | "A famous TV show just made fun of this product..." | Jenny's first frames on recognisable places and brands (section 7) **[PRACT]**; the house rule: rules/SHORTS.md |
| Visual payoff at frame 0, explained after | The thing itself on screen, the voice explains | Jenny; Demand Curve **[PRACT]** |
| Open loop + foreshadow of the ending | "...and the last one is the reason the company..." | Jenny's hook + foreshadow **[PRACT]** |
| Counted mechanism ("3 things") | The viewer knows how far along they are | Jenny: "very easy to follow ... the viewer knows ... we're getting closer" **[PRACT]**; a top creator we studied: counted "3 Things..." Shorts at 1.1-1.4M views **[OURS]** |
| "What happens if" experiment | Test a claim on screen, result at the end | A top creator we studied: their top "What happens if..." Short at 9.4M views **[OURS]** |

**Text at frame 0:** the outside advice is a separate text hook on top ("slap that context right at the top", heyDominik **[PRACT]**). The
house standard has no static text layer and no text that repeats the voice (standard/SHORTS.md, rules/SHORTS.md), so there the hook's key
word arrives as the hero caption on the spoken word, centred, and the picture carries the context. Where the two differ, the house
standard wins.

## 3. Pacing: cuts, motion, B-roll density

### 3.1 Measured
| Set | Cuts per minute | Shot length | Other | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Batch 1 (2 top Shorts we studied) | not recorded | median 1.4-1.6 s, p90 ~3.3 s | 223 / 203 wpm | **[OURS]** |
| Batch 2 (5 Shorts) | 23-47 | median 0.80-1.98 s | 69% of one music-driven Short's cuts on the beat (chance 24%) | **[OURS]** |
| Batch 3 (9 Shorts) | **43 median (20-78)** | **median 1.1 s (0.77-2.35)** | camera moving in **80%** of frames; one Short pushes 52% of its shots by 1.32x | **[OURS]** |
| 50 high-performing talking-head TikToks (non-English) | **22 mean (0.37 cuts/s, SD 0.17; 8-49)**; 16th/50th/84th pct 11 / 20 / 31 | n/a | PySceneDetect, the same cut detector we use | [Dost & Huang, 2026](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2026/pages/PDF/paper_professor_DOST_HUANG.pdf) **[STUDY]** |

- **The pace ceiling (the one experiment found).** Same 20-s talking-head clip in 7 edits, N = 242: "Higher pacing reduced sustained
  engagement overall"; "avoid chasing speed for its own sake" ([Dost & Huang, 2026](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2026/pages/PDF/paper_professor_DOST_HUANG.pdf)).
  **[STUDY]**, with limits: a convenience sample, self-reported intent, jump cuts in one talking head, not B-roll montage. Read it as: cuts
  that add nothing cost attention; it does not argue against new pictures that each carry a word.
- **Vendor rule of thumb:** "one cut every 2-4 seconds" (OpusClip, 2025-11-11) **[VENDOR]**, slower than every measured set above.

### 3.2 How the good ones cut (a frame-by-frame study of top Shorts) **[OURS]**
- "No cut lands on a timer. Every one lands on a meaning": the picture arrives on the word that names it ("Einstein" -> Einstein photo).
- Variety comes from different sources, never the same picture twice inside an idea.
- Motion lives inside the shots (the footage's own camera, slow pushes on stills); shots hold 1.5-2.5 s only when they move by themselves.
- Transitions are rare and sit on turns (zoom blur into the first footage shot, a light leak at the turn, an iris out on the last word).

### 3.3 The house pace rules
The longest a shot may hold without a cut, cut-away or punch-in (checked by `tools/motion/qa_holds.py`), a new matching picture on every
cut, more cuts instead of a clip slowed to fill its shot, list montages cut on each word, and calm footage under talking: the house
standard sets these (standard/SHORTS.md, rules/SHORTS.md). One observed failure behind them: a cut-away that returned to the same picture
(A, B, A) did not count as a new picture.

## 4. Captions

### 4.1 Measured in the reference batches **[OURS]**
- **1-3 words, mid-screen (0.50-0.62 of the height), in 9 of 9** (batch 3). Batch 1: 1 word (sometimes 2), key words coloured.
- Word-by-word build: each word fades in over 50-100 ms, 2-4 words per chunk, old chunk cross-fades out in ~130 ms (two of the batch).
- Font and colour follow the subject and stay fixed for the whole Short (a fantasy serif for a fantasy topic, neon green for science).
- Pop-in curve that reads as premium: 0.83 -> 1.07 in 4 frames with blur, settled by 200 ms.
- Swear words starred in the caption.

### 4.2 The house caption rules, and the phone measurement
- The caption spec (1:1 with the audio, size, position, punctuation, key-word colour, no second text layer, where emphasis lives): the
  house standard sets it (standard/SHORTS.md, rules/SHORTS.md).
- **Measured by us on a phone [OURS]:** on an iPhone, YouTube shows only about **x 50-1030** of the 1080-px frame, so every text and logo
  stays inside it; text placed behind the iPhone status bar was unreadable. Check text on the phone itself.

### 4.3 Outside claims
- **UI safe zones: no official YouTube spec found.** Vendor numbers disagree: e.g. top 120 px, bottom 300 px (360 with the description
  open), right 96 px on 1080x1920 ([adaptlypost, undated, no method](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide)); other
  guides reserve up to 672 px at the bottom. **[VENDOR]** Centred captions and the x 50-1030 phone measurement make these moot for captions;
  they matter for anything placed low or right (the subscribe row and the like/comment column).
- **"Captions raise retention 15-25%" (OpusClip) and "animated captions beat static ~2x" (ClipSpeed): no sample, no method.** No controlled
  caption test was found. **[VENDOR]**, do not cite as fact.
- **Emojis in captions: no evidence found either way**, and the reference batches did not record any. Unverified; not a practice to add.

## 5. Sound: music, SFX, voice

### 5.1 Rules of the platform (measured costs) **[YT]**
- **A Short over one minute with an active copyright claim of any type is blocked worldwide**: not playable, not recommended, not monetized
  until resolved; under one minute nothing changed ([YouTube Help, "Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en);
  [Music eligibility for Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873?hl=en)). This covers any claimed audio or footage, not only music.
- Shorts Audio Library songs: most usable up to 90 s in a 3-minute Short, some capped at 60 or 30 s; YouTube Audio Library tracks "won't
  receive a Content ID claim" (same pages).
- Shorts that use the same sound are grouped on a sound page; tapping "Sound" in the player links to the source and other Shorts using it
  ([YouTube Help 10623810 via search snippet](https://pss.support.google.com/youtube/answer/10623810)). Not read in full.

### 5.2 Trends vs a house sound
- Jordan Howlett with YouTube's Todd Sherman ([2025-07-31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LsJD6qPBP5o)) **[PRACT/YT]**: trends are "a fantastic
  tool", "especially for newer creators", but a trend-bound video "doesn't stay current"; Sherman: a trend works as a shared moment, "a
  little bit like an inside joke", and "sometimes it's not even a shared sound. Sometimes it's just the concept."
- A top creator we studied (the biggest news channel of the niche we studied) runs the same song under every Short: a house sound.
  **[OURS]** Which music a Short uses: standard/MUSIC.md.

### 5.3 Measured in the reference batches **[OURS]**
- **Bed 10-20 LU under the voice** (median ~13), ducked ~7 dB under speech; Shorts beds are far louder than long form (~22 LU).
- **SFX go on visible events, not on plain cuts:** plain cuts carry a sound at chance rate in 8 of 9 (batch 3) and in 3 of 3 (batch 2).
  8-15 SFX a minute in news-type Shorts, each illustrating its word (our own Shorts ran ~25/min).
- **Timing:** hits peak +20 to +30 ms after the picture's first frame (median +27 ms, n = 14); whooshes start 70-320 ms before the cut
  and peak 50-110 ms after it.
- **Music-driven pieces cut on the beat** (one: 69% of cuts on the beat vs 24% chance; another: 54% of cuts on bass onsets); narrated
  pieces cut on words, at chance against the beat.
- **Devices:** silence punchline (bed off 1.5-2 s after the setup, slammed back on the next cut); an 8 dB dip ~1.3 s before a reveal word.
- **Mix:** -15.2 / -15.3 LUFS (batch 1).

### 5.4 Voice pace
- **Measured: 203-232 wpm** in the reference batches (batch 1: 223 and 203; batch 3 median 232), no pause over ~0.14 s **[OURS]**.
- Vendor planning figures of 140-180 wpm for Shorts narration (e.g. [flowshorts](https://flowshorts.app/blog/words-per-minute-speaking)) are
  **below what the references measure** **[VENDOR]**.
- The house voice rules for Shorts (the longest gap, one speed with the hook included, no pitch-shifting): standard/SOUND_AND_VOICE.md and
  rules/SHORTS.md.

## 6. Endings, loops, CTA and length

### 6.1 Endings and loops
- **Every second at the end counts (measured by a creator).** Jenny Hoyos saw a 25-point drop in the last second of a Short (70% -> 45%),
  trimmed that one second in Studio, and average % viewed went 83% -> 88% "and the video went flying" ([Creator Science, 2023-10](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)). **[PRACT]**
- **End abruptly on the high.** "Whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it" (Jenny, same). "It ends abruptly ...
  if you linger after the high note, people won't re-watch" ([Demand Curve, 2024-06-11](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-187)). **[PRACT]**
- **Loops raise % viewed past 100%.** Jenny: her 85% stay rate against 95% retention is only possible "because they're rewatching it". An
  animator's seamless-loop Short went from 96.8% to 187.1% average viewed ([vidIQ, 2021-05-11](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/)). **[PRACT/DATA]**
- **Ours:** a Short built as a loop (the last sentence runs into the hook; the last frame is the take just before the opening frame) reached
  102.7-119% average viewed. **[OURS]** In batch 2, none of the 5 scripted a loop (the music-driven one loops by construction). **[OURS]**
- **CTA:** put any ask mid-video or make it subtle; "a boring CTA at the end prevents" the re-watch (Demand Curve, 2024-06-11) **[PRACT]**.
  1 of the 5 batch-2 Shorts had a CTA (last 3.8 s) **[OURS]**. A Short can carry a **related video** link to a long video (YouTube Help lists
  "Add a related video to your YouTube Shorts", [Help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)) **[YT]**: a bridge
  to long form that costs no seconds of the Short.

### 6.2 Length
| Source | Finding | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube, Todd Sherman, 2023-08-23 | "To what length do you need to tell your story? As opposed to trying to target an exact length" | **[YT]** |
| Paddy Galloway / Chris Gileta, 5,400 Shorts, 33 channels (reported 2023-04-25, pre-3-minute era) | 50-60 s got the most views on average; most creators made 20-40 s | **[DATA]** |
| quso.ai, 108,138 matured Shorts (updated 2026-06-29) | Median views by length: **<10 s 514 · 11-20 s 901 (peak) · 31-45 s 347 · 46-60 s 188 · >90 s 51** ([quso](https://quso.ai/research/youtube-shorts-length)) | **[DATA]**, clip-tool users, medians under 1K views |
| Jenny Hoyos, 2023-10 | Her best are "exactly 34 seconds"; YouTube's audience wants "a slower pace, more story" than TikTok (10-20 s there) | **[PRACT]** |
| Our reference batches (14 top Shorts, batches 2+3) | Median ~28 s (19-46 s) | **[OURS]** |
| The house standard | How length is set: rules/SHORTS.md | |
| 3-minute Shorts | **No performance study found.** Measured cost: any claim blocks a Short over 60 s | **[YT]** |

**Reasoning (ours):** the two big datasets disagree (50-60 s best in 2023 big-channel data; 11-20 s best in 2026 small-creator medians), so
length is not a lever by itself. The lever is the payoff density per second. For a Short built on other parties' footage the practical cap
is **60 s** because of the claim rule (a maker's official videos and outlets' footage can carry claims).

## 7. Framing: 9:16 from 16:9

- **The house framing rules** (the subject of the line centred, vertically too; every crop checked on a sheet; the screen filled, no blurred
  bars, no sharp strip in a blurred field; split screens edge to edge, meeting in the middle, captions on the picture; one clean centred
  thing on screen, never clogged; premium assets with depth, no cheap pop-ins): standard/SHORTS.md and rules/SHORTS.md. One observed
  failure behind them: an unchecked 9:16 crop of a 16:9 frame showed a lamp post and cut both people and the logo out.
- **The reference batches [OURS]:** pictures come in three ways: a full-screen swap, on top of the running clip (no blur behind it, 0 of 9 blur
  it), or a split (the clip keeps the top half, the picture slams into the bottom from ~1.4x in ~0.17 s or slides in ~0.3 s), captions on the
  seam (three of the batch). A top creator we studied posted a landscape Short on a blurred fill: the lowest views per subscriber in the
  batch (0.13x), one data point.
- **Outside:** "Shorts that don't fill the entire mobile screen feel amateurish" (OpusClip **[VENDOR]**); faces and text in the middle third
  (same). Jenny's first frames use recognisable places and brands so a stranger "knew those faces/logos more than her" **[PRACT]**.
- **Phone check, measured:** iPhone shows x ~50-1030 (section 4.2); keep the subject's key detail away from the outer 50 px.

## 8. What differs from long form (the swipe vs the click)

| | Long form | Shorts | Source |

**The psychology in one line (our reasoning from the sources):** a long-form viewer has already paid with a click and gives the video
minutes to earn it; a Shorts viewer has paid nothing and leaves the instant the next swipe looks better. So a Short has no setup time,
no repeat picture, no dead second at the end, and its first frame has to work as a thumbnail with the sound off.

## 10. Measuring a reference batch (the method)

Build a batch of about 15 Shorts: the creator's own references first where they fit, then the closest formats to the channel's, then one
genre outlier for hooks (measure only its first 2 s and its ending). Measure each one: cuts (PySceneDetect), shot lengths, first word,
first cut, motion (share of frames where the camera moves, push amounts), caption position and size, SFX per visible event, bed LU, wpm,
longest pause, and last-second behaviour (does it loop, where does it end). Add a sheet at 4 frames a second with the words under it and
read it frame by frame: that sheet is what showed every cut landing on a meaning (section 3.2), which no count shows.

## 11. Checklist: 23 practices the evidence supports

*Tag = where it comes from. Where the house standard (standard/SHORTS.md, rules/SHORTS.md) sets a practice, it wins over any outside
advice; the house rules live there, not here.*

**Before the edit**
1. Write the hook, the two-line foreshadow (3 s or less) and the last line before anything else (Jenny [PRACT]).
2. The hook works with the sound off: what's on screen at 0.0-1.0 s says what the Short is about (Jenny, Todd Sherman [PRACT/YT]).
3. Lead with a name, place or brand people know, never "a product" or "some people" (Jenny's recognisable first frames [PRACT]).
4. The payoff is the last line: hook, foreshadow, then the payoff at the end (Jenny; Demand Curve [PRACT]).
5. Read the script at a 5th-grade level or below; cut any line the picture already says (Jenny; Demand Curve [PRACT]).
6. Length follows the story (Todd Sherman [YT]); cap at 60 s while any clip could carry a claim (YouTube Help [YT]).

**The first two seconds**
7. The voice starts at 0.00 s (5 of 5 references [OURS]).
8. First cut by ~1.1 s and the first visual payoff by 2.4 s (reference medians [OURS]).

**The middle**
9. A new, matching picture on the word that names it; never the same picture twice inside one idea (frame-by-frame study [OURS]).
10. Short shots: reference medians 1.1-1.6 s, p90 ~3.3 s, 43 cuts a minute median [OURS].
11. Every shot moves (push 1.1-1.3x or the footage's own camera); 80% of reference frames move [OURS].
12. No cut for its own sake: speed that adds nothing lowers engagement (Dost & Huang [STUDY]).
13. Re-hook once in the middle with a "but" or a restated premise, never "let's get started" (Jenny; Demand Curve [PRACT]).
14. Transitions only on turns (the hook into fact 1, the last fact into the end) (frame-by-frame study [OURS]).

**Captions and framing**
15. 1-3 words a caption, mid-screen (0.50-0.62 of the height); font and colour fixed for the whole Short (9 of 9 references [OURS]).
16. Fill the 9:16 frame (OpusClip [VENDOR]; the blurred-fill counter-example [OURS]); the subject centred vertically too; check every crop
    on a sheet; keep text within x 50-1030 (our phone measurement [OURS]).

**Sound**
17. SFX on visible events (pops, hero words, reveals), 8-15 a minute, not on plain cuts; hits +27 ms after the picture; whooshes start
    70-320 ms before the cut (reference batches [OURS]).
18. Bed ~13 LU under the voice (10-20), ducked ~7 dB under speech (reference batches [OURS]).
19. The references talk at 203-232 wpm with no pause over ~0.14 s (reference batches [OURS]).
20. Music-driven pieces cut on the beat; narrated pieces cut on words (reference batches [OURS]).

**The end**
21. End on the payoff word; no tail, no lingering second; check the last-second drop in the retention graph and trim if it falls (Jenny [PRACT]).
22. Loop where the story allows: the last line runs into the hook, the last frame precedes the first (our looped Short at 102.7-119% [OURS];
    vidIQ [PRACT/DATA]).
23. No end-card CTA; if a long video fits, link it as the Short's related video instead (Demand Curve [PRACT]; YouTube Help [YT]).

**Set by the house standard, not restated here** (standard/SHORTS.md, rules/SHORTS.md, standard/SOUND_AND_VOICE.md): what the hook's
first words do and which names lead, wording that stays timeless, frame 0 (the flash open and its sound), the hook's key word as the hero
caption with the drop zoom, the longest hold (`tools/motion/qa_holds.py`), calm footage under talking, one picture per list item cut on
each word with a whoosh, no text layer that repeats the voice, the caption spec (size, position, punctuation, colour), and the voice's
speed and longest gap.

**After upload (measure, don't guess):** compare each Short's stayed-to-watch rate with the channel's own typical rate (Studio's "compared
to your typical performance") and the 70-80% marks in 1.4; when it's low, the first 2 seconds get the next change, not the middle (1of10 [DATA]).
