# The script: the casebook

Every verdict of the creator on the words goes here: how a line sounds, what it names, the facts as said, the structure, the hook and
the ending, the titles. A clause that confuses the viewer is also a coherence case (`COHERENCE.md`). Read in every script round and
every voice round. Method and entry format: `README.md`. The seed lines below are the starting taste; the creator's cases join them
under each heading and replace any line they contradict.

## Sounds like a person, not like AI
**SC1** FLAGGED, again and again across formats: AI tells: the self-answered "X? Y." beat; stock phrases ("everyone's losing their
minds", "and honestly,", "that's the energy", "the whole internet instantly went"); tidy closers ("and in my opinion, they just got it
wrong", "I understand where that comes from"); "my favorite line was"; "and no, I'm not saying that to bait you"; "literally" in every
other line; a formal phrase inside a casual line -> the line: a confident creator talking to a friend, in the niche's own plain words,
with none of these tics. A question put to the viewer is fine; the self-answered beat is the tell. A phrasing once corrected never comes
back.

**SC2** FLAGGED: "kind of hilarious" with a voiced laugh on a fact that wasn't funny; a weak joke attempt; a hesitant aside ("you know
what I mean"); a joke nobody gets -> the line: humour only where it fits, current and natural; a funny fact is stated plainly as a fact;
a joke that needs explaining is cut, and the line ends on the sentence before it.

**SC3** FLAGGED: narration that sounded like a narrator, not a creator; scripts that read as machine-written -> the line: wording is
learned by listening to the creator and to the niche's best creators (their vocabulary and sentence structure, never copied), never by
inventing "cool" lines; the creator's own sample lines are lessons, not templates (a claim backed casually with its proof, "I'm not just
saying that, because ...", is one such lesson).

**SC4** PASSED: a natural reaction line ("but I don't know why you would"); current slang in its place ("lowkey my favourite") -> the
line: lines like these.

**SC5** FLAGGED: "unless you did it like I did" (boastful); a line rewritten to carry a chill "including me" -> the line: no
self-praise; the creator includes themselves lightly.

**SC7** FLAGGED: one phrase ("millions of people") three times close together -> the line: repeats aren't banned, but a term is varied
once the repeat is noticeable.

## Name the thing, plainly
**SC10** FLAGGED, the most repeated note of all: vagueness: "a real city" for the named city, "a team" for the named team, "a company in
the US", "one song" for the named song, "a product called X", "the biggest comedy shows", "a bridge that isn't even finished yet" for the
one everyone knows, "a man" for the known inventor, "the same month as X" -> the line: name it: the city, the song, the company,
the person, the month. Specific names make a claim credible; vague ones sound made up.

**SC11** FLAGGED: words too complex for a Short; a script assuming viewers know how a thing works; the niche's long word where everyone
uses a short one -> the line: words a kid and an adult both get; background given in seconds; the word people actually use.

**SC12** FLAGGED: a draft too detailed for its simple topic; a technical detail told the nerd way -> the line: for the mainstream, leave
out the nerd detail and tell what matters to the viewer; a detail that only sounds smart is made to sound crazy, or cut. Generalise by
leaving detail out, never by going vague (SC10 still holds).

**SC13** FLAGGED: an understated number ("two billion, if you're being generous"); numbers a casual viewer can't grasp -> the line: the
higher true number and the most exciting true framing, in numbers a casual viewer can hold (rounded, said like a person).

## Facts as said
**SC20** FLAGGED: invented or misread details: an object in a hand misread, a place named wrong, a meaning invented for a shot, an
animal named from a file name instead of the picture -> the line: one invented or misread detail costs more than any good line; every
detail comes from the picture or the primary source, checked.

**SC21** FLAGGED: "an outlet spotted it" for something everyone saw; "somebody actually counted, and there are at least four" over a
picture where the four are plain to see -> the line: an obvious thing is said as obvious, never dressed up as somebody's find; no "at
least" on a number you can see.

**SC22** FLAGGED: random social accounts quoted by name as if they were authorities; PASSED: "an account posted" with no name -> the
line: a random account is no authority.

**SC23** FLAGGED: a hedge kept from the fact check ("at least that's what they're called for now"); PASSED: the line cut from the voice
-> the line: a doubt about a source never goes into the voice as a hedge; the fact is said straight or left out.

**SC24** FLAGGED: a feature stated as confirmed when it could be an unfinished prototype; "the maker just confirmed" in a hook when
they hadn't -> the line: a theory is fine, said as a theory; nothing unconfirmed is stated as fact.

**SC25** FLAGGED: features the previous version already had, listed as what makes the new one special -> the line: in a guide to what's
new, a feature the last version had is never sold as new; only the new part is said.

**SC26** FLAGGED: our own check coming up empty, told as content ("I went through more than three hundred older ones and couldn't find a
single one") -> the line: what we checked and didn't find is no information for a viewer; the video tells what is there and what it
could mean.

**SC27** FLAGGED: "nothing is official yet", "the episode drops today", "just got cancelled" about old news -> the line: timeless wording:
no "just", "yesterday", "today", "tomorrow"; dates are fine.

**SC28** FLAGGED: old news everyone knows recited as the video; a known thing sold as news -> the line: known things are said plainly,
as background; news is only the reference for the claim, and the narration covers what is new.

**SC29** FLAGGED: details nobody cares about (a person's hair length); filler lines that add nothing ("full chaos"); a boring fact; a
fact that isn't about the subject itself -> the line: every line adds something the viewer cares about, about the subject itself.

**SC30** FLAGGED: a plan to show an article on screen and read it out; PASSED: the chat's own find of where each picture of a set was
taken -> the line: an article's text is paraphrased, with only a few lines quoted; a Short takes its essentials, a long video takes the
whole article apart; sharp niche knowledge shows (details only a real follower spots).

## Every sentence makes sense alone
**SC35** FLAGGED: a definition wedged between a subject and its verb ("until the sommelier, that's the person who picks the wine,
opens the bottle"); a first fix that cut the definition missed the point -> the line: the clause stays whole and the explanation follows
as its own sentence ("until the sommelier opens the bottle. That's the person who picks the wine.").

**SC36** FLAGGED: a sentence hard to follow; an unclear "it"; a sentence with no clear message; a past tense that confused the ear; the
wrong connector ("but"); an incoherent last sentence -> the line: every sentence, read alone as a normal person hears it, makes sense.

**SC37** FLAGGED: an opener whose turn ("and nothing you don't") landed too late to parse -> the line: a line parses on first hearing.

**SC38** FLAGGED: items explained out of the order they appear on screen, one of them skipped -> the line: in the order shown, none
skipped.

## Long form: the story
**SC40** FLAGGED: a version that reported old news instead of the title's promise; an opening that felt like marketing for the subject
-> the line: start from the title's promise (what a mainstream viewer expects from that title); the promise is the spine.

**SC41** FLAGGED: a proposal for a future-tense title that only looked back, with no predictions or theories; PASSED: the next proposal,
built on predictions -> the line: a future-tense title makes the video about what will change: predictions and theories at its core,
reaching with confidence instead of scratching the surface.

**SC42** FLAGGED: a strong example from the past left standing on its own instead of built up into what it means for the subject -> the
line: specifics lead to general conclusions, never a messy list; new information is woven into the structure, not tacked on.

**SC43** FLAGGED: a video planned around a target length; many of the creator's talked-through points and their own take left out -> the
line: the length follows the story, never a minute target, and content is never squeezed to shorten. The creator's own noticed moments
and opinions are the base; the chat fills the rest with its own finds and expounds most on the moments that stand out.

**SC44** FLAGGED (an argument video): a claim our own clips contradicted; a weak argument; an ironic line that sounded sincere; an
ending on needless numbers -> the line: show each claim, then disprove it with the source's own clips; the ending lands on the video's
point.

**SC45** FLAGGED (an argument video): a rebuttal argued on a side point when the real point was the cherry-picking of clips; PASSED: the
chat's own proposal to understand the other side instead of insulting it -> the line: to expose cherry-picking, show the clips left out
and the reversed scenario; understand the other side's reasons and still make confident calls; no insulting labels.

**SC46** FLAGGED: the subscribe ask placed before a list, where it sounded like the end; the ask forced in mid-argument, splitting one
point -> the line: the ask sits at a real break between points, never where it sounds like the end.

**SC47** FLAGGED: an abrupt ending; a generic sign-off -> the line: a like-and-comment sign-off written for this video, then stop; the
video keeps a set opener and sign-off phrase.

**SC48** FLAGGED (a mystery video): jokes, puns, coincidence-hunting and reactions told beside the serious readings; PASSED, after the
creator's first doubt: a reading built on something real the source showed (a symbol carved into a ship's mast) -> the line: a mystery video takes
only the theories something real stands behind and people mean; the tone is serious. The creator's last word on a line is the verdict,
not their first doubt.

**SC49** FLAGGED: unrealistic questions put in a beginner's mouth -> the line: questions come from what real viewers ask.

**SC50** FLAGGED (a facts series): the first part stacking the ten best facts, weakening the other parts; facts framed around something
other than the subject -> the line: a series is planned as a whole: the best spread across the parts, every fact about the subject
itself, every part opening with the same quick hook, the facts numbered (viewers comment their favourite).

## Shorts: hook, flow, ending
**SC55** FLAGGED: hooks that didn't hook; boring first words; a stale hook; a hook that gave away the punchline; a hook naming what the
end reveals -> the line: the first words hook: drama, curiosity, real names people relate to, the punchline held back. The hook
promises the exact payoff; the end gives it.

**SC56** FLAGGED: the payoff a casual viewer cares about placed after the details; PASSED: line edits that held the turn back to the end
-> the line: the most interesting thing first, the truth or the turn held to the end; hold the payoff, never fill time.

**SC57** FLAGGED: a redundant restatement; unneeded facts; a detour into another topic; too much backstory before the current story ->
the line: Shorts don't stall: every line adds something new, point to point, compressed.

**SC58** FLAGGED: the arguments for and against told in two blocks -> the line: they alternate.

**SC59** FLAGGED: an ending that neither looped nor ended; "and honestly, I love it" as a closer; a weird loop line; a like-and-comment
ask breaking the loop -> the line: a clean loop (the last line flows into the first) or a comment question.

**SC60** FLAGGED: Short ideas cut from a long video that only worked with its context -> the line: a Short stands alone; it is built for
Shorts retention, never a cut-down, and Shorts taken from a long video are chosen critically.

**SC61** PASSED: a story Short told straight, its setup paid off by the real person's own words; two real bites carrying a story -> the
line: the bar for a story Short.

**SC62** FLAGGED: a Short so excited it sounded like an ad for the paid scheme it covered; a script too neutral; a debunk framing; an
angle too narrow for the story -> the line: take the viewers' side with a clear, critical stance, and frame the story at its widest.

## Stance and opinions
**SC65** FLAGGED: videos that sounded like neutral news -> the line: the channel carries opinions and theories, not a neutral news
feed; confident, never overly careful; the opinions said chill (a relaxed, neutral persona, not a shouting fan).

**SC66** FLAGGED: careful talking where a confident call-out fits; a hedged "honestly, this might be" -> the line: say it straight.

## Words that cost reach
**SC70** FLAGGED: a line describing violence against people graphically, and a sexual topic, each likely to have limited a Short's push
-> the line: word around them; avoid "kill" and words like it in the narration; a risky word is the chat's call, covered if used
(`SOUND.md` SO13).

## Ideas and angles
**SC75** FLAGGED: a derivative idea list; ideas that echo the news; PASSED: nearly every untold, story-like idea; a near-copy of a video
already made dropped -> the line: creative, timeless angles; the untold story wins; never a near-copy of our own.

**SC76** FLAGGED: a Short dropped whole after it was built, as a whole, one the creator had nearly skipped when picking it -> the line:
a pick the creator doubted is that piece's main risk, said before it is built; no reason is invented for a verdict that gave none.

## Titles
**SC80** FLAGGED: a title too niche for its concept -> the line: broad and mainstream, readable by a casual viewer, made to be the main
video on its topic.

**SC81** FLAGGED: the chat proposing to drop measured bait wording as "too baity"; a Short's title not clickbaity enough -> the line:
bait is welcome and measured bait wording stays; a word that might hurt the push stays in a title when it is what makes people click.

**SC82** FLAGGED: a first batch of title options too narrow and too alike; the titles of a series that didn't read like one -> the line:
many genuinely different options; an A/B test uses three different types of title, not three similar ones; a series keeps one title
format and one set of tags.

**SC83** FLAGGED: a title word that sank a Short (no views) -> the line: swapped for one with the same pull and no risk.

**SC84** FLAGGED: a proposed series name that sounded like the subject's own brand -> the line: our names never sound like theirs.

**SC85** FLAGGED: titles not built on the title research done before -> the line: titles come from studying the titles that worked; a
niche topic works when its title and content are interesting and clear.
