# The picture: the casebook

Every verdict of the creator on the picture goes here: the footage and its quality, framing, zoom and motion, cuts and timing, built
graphics, marks, and text on screen. What the picture shows under a line (does it match the words?) is `COHERENCE.md`. Every builder
brief points at this book; the reviewer's eye reads it before it flags anything. Method and entry format: `README.md`. The seed lines
below are the starting taste; the creator's cases join them under each heading and replace any line they contradict.

## Footage: the source and its quality
**PI1** FLAGGED: a soft, low-resolution clip or still full screen where a sharper copy existed; footage taken from a re-upload or a
social post instead of the 4K master on disk -> the line: the sharpest official copy (the maker's own media page, the master on disk),
at good resolution unless only a low-res copy exists.

**PI2** FLAGGED: footage stuttering in the render -> the line: every clip plays smooth; the render is checked for it before delivery.

**PI3** FLAGGED: a moving clip that stops on a still frame mid-motion (in the body, in the outro) -> the line: a moving clip keeps
moving; a frozen in-motion frame looks like a fault unless it is a deliberate freeze on the best frame.

**PI4** FLAGGED: generic stock (an empty office, teens on a couch, a stock phone being scrolled, a stock face for a person the words
describe); PASSED: a mix of clips and stills -> the line: real, relatable material over stock: the real app being scrolled, the niche's
known creators, the actual clip talked about. Stills are fine (video is not always better than an image; only generic stock was wrong).
Never AI-generated footage or pictures: real footage and our own graphics only.

**PI5** FLAGGED: the same picture shown twice in one video (the second time cropped and panned); the same drone shot twice within three
clips; a clip from the intro reused in the outro -> the line: never the same clip or picture twice inside a video when the material is
plentiful. Across videos, footage may repeat (the best fit wins); our sentences and graphics never do.

**PI6** FLAGGED: older footage used as filler where newer shots of the same thing exist -> the line: when several fit, the newest
material first.

**PI7** FLAGGED: a cut-out with remnants of its background along an edge -> the line: cut-outs are clean, checked at full size.

**PI8** FLAGGED: a still placed too high, a black band across the bottom ~13% of the frame -> the line: a picture that doesn't fill its
frame is a bug; the render scan and the gate catch every black band before the creator sees one.

**PI9** FLAGGED: a cover shown letterboxed inside a frame it should fill; a bar graphic showing early, then cropped; PASSED: covers that
fill their frames -> the line: a picture fills its frame whole.

**PI10** FLAGGED: a clip turned 90 degrees over a blurred copy of itself -> the line: a clip is never rotated to fit.

**PI11** FLAGGED: a news clip whose channel graphic pops in for a few frames at its start; a clip opening on a few stray frames before
its subject -> the line: a clip starts after anything that isn't its subject.

**PI12** FLAGGED: a page capture showing an "ADVERTISEMENT" label under the highlighted text -> the line: a page is captured clean.

**PI13** FLAGGED: one panel of a side-by-side too dark to see -> the line: every panel reads.

**PI14** FLAGGED: the primary source's own interface (an app's menu with its icons, its step counter, its own chart) redrawn as our own
bar or icons over a blur; a self-made chart where the real one exists; a fake error screen laid over a still instead of the real moment -> the
line: the source's own interface beats any drawn stand-in; it is drawn only if no frame has it. A count the words name (three tabs,
four badges) is shown, the real indicator first.

**PI15** FLAGGED: no official picture of an app the words describe existed, and a loose stand-in picture was used -> the line: build it
in our own phone device; reuse a device the creator already liked.

**PI16** FLAGGED: two people shown as oddly picked circle profile pictures -> the line: a portrait is the best picture of the person for
that format, picked once and used everywhere.

**PI17** FLAGGED: a detail (an engraving on a ring) shown on an official still taken side-on, picked as the clearest official view, while the clip
right before it shows the detail face-on -> the line: the clearest picture is judged by how it reads on screen, face-on and big, never
by its source's prestige or file size. When the clip the line names shows the thing clearly, freeze that clip and zoom in (a 4K master
frame cut in exactly where the clip leaves off, so the zoom stays sharp) instead of cutting away to a side-on still. PASSED: the fix.

**PI18** (Shorts) PASSED: old 720p-1080p footage when every line still gets its own picture; a real object from outside the niche (a
lighthouse, its lamp going dark) as an act-out; a low-res real clip shown as a card; an inset of a clip's own phone -> the line: real
material passes at the resolution it exists in, as long as each line has its picture.

**PI19** (Shorts) FLAGGED: a speaker's own low-res talk held ~7 s on one slide with no cut (the render scan had failed it and the fail
was waved through as the speaker's own clip) -> the line: in a Short a bite is cut like the rest: a new, crisp picture of what they say
every 2-3 s. A render scan's fail is never waved through by an older rule: the scan saw exactly what the creator saw.

## Framing, zoom and motion
**PI20** FLAGGED: two people simply sitting there, shown cropped in on their faces; an official still opened 1.5x cropped and panned
to another crop while the whole picture already showed what the line says -> the line: a picture is shown whole, at its full
resolution, with a little motion; a crop or zoom only where the words point at a detail the whole frame can't show.

**PI21** FLAGGED: shots opening already zoomed in; PASSED: the very slow zoom that keeps a frame in motion -> the line: the full picture
first, then the move onto its subject, centred; never open on a crop, and zoom only where it is fully coherent. The slow keep-alive
drift is fine.

**PI22** FLAGGED: a zoom meant for a detail that lands on nothing beside it -> the line: every zoom lands on its named thing, centred.

**PI23** FLAGGED: too many and too long zoom-ins on screenshots, staying close-up too long, softness from the zoom -> the line: a zoom
is brief and onto its thing.

**PI24** FLAGGED: a two-stage zoom; a zoom out and back in where a simple pan down would show the thing -> the line: one calm move from
the starting point.

**PI25** FLAGGED and PASSED: numbers popping in on their words (fine) over a still re-framed four times (never) -> the line: items
landing on their words are fine; the ground jumping between framings is not: one calm move.

**PI26** FLAGGED: an off-centre framing; a zoom onto a sign that sat off-centre and cut it off -> the line: centred, always.

**PI27** FLAGGED: a random zoom plus a sound on an action shot -> the line: a zoom only when it makes sense.

**PI28** FLAGGED: a slow push that pushed the thing the line was about (a label on a jar) out of the frame -> the line: a move never
pushes the subject out.

**PI29** FLAGGED: a still that looked frozen, with nothing moving; a left-right drift that wasn't smooth -> the line: a still gets a
smooth keep-alive drift.

**PI30** FLAGGED: two cards side by side shaking under a keep-alive push (each edge stepping 1 px on different frames) -> the line:
nothing on screen ever shakes; a card that moves or zooms moves as one picture (laid out in constant pixels, moved by one transform).

**PI31** FLAGGED: a card's right and bottom edges glitching (its last pixel column showing undimmed) -> the line: a card is clean to its
last pixel on every frame, in every instance; a dim reaches past the picture and the card clips it.

**PI32** FLAGGED: a clip slowed down with frame interpolation (it broke into blocks); a clip replayed right after it had played -> the
line: a clip plays at its own speed; never interpolated slow motion, never a straight repeat of what was just seen.

**PI33** PASSED: a moment played slowed, then frozen and explained; FLAGGED: clips slowed to fill a shot (one looked like 3 fps); slow
motion with no reason -> the line: slow motion is a deliberate replay that shows a moment better; a shot that outlasts its clip gets a
second clip, never a slow-down.

**PI34** PASSED: a sped-up transition clip; FLAGGED: the clip staying sped up after it lands on its subject -> the line: back to normal
speed on the landing.

**PI35** FLAGGED: a frozen, zoomed, dark frame under a line about an action, hard to make out -> the line: a moving clip beats a freeze,
and the whole picture beats a crop.

## Cuts, timing and holds
**PI40** FLAGGED: one or two frames of the neighbouring clip at a cut (stray frames, flash frames, a one-frame sliver), found in review
after review -> the line: none, ever; every cut is checked frame by frame.

**PI41** FLAGGED, twice in one watch: a clip on screen ~0.5 s between two longer shots, too short for anyone to notice -> the line: no
clip under ~1 s outside a deliberate rapid-fire run; the shot before plays on instead.

**PI42** FLAGGED: one frame held 5 s; one still for a whole section with nothing moving; a text card held far too long -> the line: a
still is a beat (~3.5 s, one purposeful move at most); nothing is held past its point. Clips under a line are fine; a static card held
long isn't.

**PI43** FLAGGED: a clip running on past its words -> the line: each clip ends with its words.

**PI44** FLAGGED: the next clip starting under the tail of the sentence before; the old clip staying on for most of a new sentence ->
the line: a picture changes on the first word of its own sentence or clause, never under the tail of the one before.

**PI45** (long form) FLAGGED: clips constantly cutting, repeating and looping; clips frozen for no reason, too often; a clip played a
moment, then frozen and held; a frame frozen halfway through a fade; a freeze chosen too late, the sign it was for already cut off; the
picture frozen while the clip's audio played on; PASSED: a scene played out, then frozen on its best frame and explained calmly -> the
line: in a breakdown a clip plays once at its own speed, then, only when there is something to look at, one calm freeze on its best
frame. Not every clip, and not a rule to follow to the letter; the clip's audio stops with its picture.

**PI46** (long form) FLAGGED: a 15 s still with music after the last ask; a film ending abruptly on a frozen frame; a jump cut into a
big end card -> the line: after the sign-off the video fades out at once; it fades in from black and out to black.

**PI47** FLAGGED: a black-screen beat that felt like a mistake -> the line: a black screen or a join reads as intended.

**PI48** FLAGGED: a graphic's dark ground alone for 0.7 s before its first tile came in, a critical failure for retention -> the line:
nothing on screen for more than ~0.5 s, and only if its thing pops in within that time; PASSED as the fix: all tiles in on the first
word, dimmed until named.

**PI49** FLAGGED: frame 0 an empty road with sky and hills under the opening words, though it was the exact picture for them -> the
line: frame 0 has a subject the eye lands on at once, big enough, lit and moving; never an empty road or vista.

**PI50** FLAGGED: a chapter opening on a still where an exciting clip of the same thing exists -> the line: a section opens on its most
exciting real moment, a clip.

**PI51** FLAGGED: a flash montage with unequal screen times and repeated pictures -> the line: equal times, no repeats.

**PI52** FLAGGED: an edit that was correct but boring: slow pans on stills, a new clip every 2 s, flat pop-in cards, plain cuts -> the
line: boring is a fault; the edit is current and professional, at the quality of the best creators studied (their techniques understood,
never copied 1:1).

**PI53** PASSED as a direction, with issues left: a rework where every claim got its demonstration on screen (a built graphic for each
rule, the statement's words stamped, every cut on its words), its remaining issues only in one graphic's execution and in how long the
words stayed -> the line: what is said is demonstrated on screen; that kind of answer is the right one, and is then judged on its
execution.

**PI54** PASSED in one round, uploaded as it was: a full film whose every earlier note had been fixed across its whole class, not one by
one -> the line: notes are fixed as classes; once the creator passes it, no further check runs on the export.

## Built graphics and animation
**PI60** FLAGGED: real things drawn from shapes: a ring of coloured shapes, boats as circles on a harbour diagram, an object as a flat
drawing passed between two circles, an icon that doesn't read as its thing, a real device rebuilt from invented shapes on a black void;
PASSED: the same scene rebuilt with real-looking 3D models, light and motion -> the line: a real thing is a real-looking asset or a
real cut-out (full-size cut-outs of the two people, the real object travelling from one to the other), never random shapes or random
PNGs moving; the bar is professional digital animation.

**PI61** FLAGGED: crowded graphics (circles, chips, a scale), a messy days grid, an off-centre graph -> the line: one idea per moment,
centred, big; simple and clean beats complete.

**PI62** PASSED: a built graphic of meters over a week, clear, centred and on its words -> the line: a built graphic like this stays.

**PI63** FLAGGED: circles of cropped screenshots in dozens of graphics, after one passed piece had used them once -> the line: a passed
piece is a level, never a device to copy; praise for one move is not a template.

**PI64** FLAGGED: a small pop-up that showed exactly what the words described (a heart over a kiss) removed after a note about random
PNGs -> the line: an illustration that shows the words is not a random PNG. Before removing something the creator passed, check their
reason covers it; a technique they praised stays through a restyle.

**PI65** FLAGGED: the cheap look: flat cards, dotted maps, a confetti effect reading as white stripes, a cramped tips layout, an unpolished
question card, a cheap clock, home-made social-post cards, a fake stamp shown for a split second, ugly graphs and charts; PASSED in the
same videos: the pop-ins, the grids, the timeline's technique -> the line: nothing may look cheap; the look is premium and designed. The
look and the technique are judged apart: a technique can be top tier while its look fails.

**PI66** FLAGGED: map graphics: three maps thrown over each other, overlapping; six label boxes with two pairs overlapping; a big claim
over a busy satellite map with stacked chips; a home-made map with pale hairline outlines and chips -> the line: a map graphic is
designed: a calm base, filled shapes at true scale fitted like puzzle pieces, labels inside their areas, one clean statement; or real
footage of the places.

**PI67** FLAGGED: a prop tiny in the middle of the blur -> the line: a prop is sized to read at a glance (about a third of the frame).

**PI68** FLAGGED: a 6x glide into a corner element of a 4K frame, centred through a black pad, its box 150 px off the target, other
elements sharing the crop, soft at that zoom; PASSED as the fix: the element rebuilt crisp (as the source draws it) in the middle of the
frame over the picture blurred back, the statement under it, held ~1.5 s -> the line: a graphic is centred, whole, crisp at its size and
holds only its subject; a measured box is checked on its frame before use. The fix is the bar for every graphic of a film.

**PI69** FLAGGED: a "no" drawn as a red diagonal stroke over a greyed tile -> the line: a "no" is a clean, modern device.

**PI70** FLAGGED: a change between two artworks shown with drawn white copies of the changed things gliding over the second -> the line:
both real pictures whole, side by side; the changed thing cut out of the first (its own pixels) travels visibly to the second, lands
where it sat in the first, then moves and scales onto its place in the second until they overlap exactly.

**PI71** FLAGGED: a reused animation broken on its new timings (one planet drawn inside another) -> the line: a reused animation gets its
numbers checked on its new words.

**PI72** FLAGGED: an effect against its word's meaning (a rewind effect on "come back"); a prepared effect forced in where it doesn't
fit; a weak shake for "blew up" -> the line: an effect only where its meaning fits; "blew up" gets a real explosion.

**PI73** FLAGGED: a fire effect played too fast, then, slowed, looking low-fps against the rest -> the line: an effect runs at the
video's own frame rate and its natural speed.

**PI74** FLAGGED: letterbox bars dropping out and re-animating for no reason; an unexplained black bar on a screen -> the line: nothing
comes or goes without a reason.

**PI75** FLAGGED: a calendar graphic over footage that already shows the date -> the line: no graphic repeats what the picture shows.

**PI76** FLAGGED: a phone overlay badly placed (a good idea, poorly executed); a speech-bubble tail miscoloured and misplaced; a split
graphic off-centre with its bar over the person; a glow effect cropped and out of place -> the line: placement is exact, checked on its
frames.

**PI77** FLAGGED: a reference channel's own device copied (its desktop-window date shot) -> the line: references set the quality bar;
the specifics are ours.

**PI78** FLAGGED: the wrong version of a company's logo -> the line: the official asset.

**PI79** PASSED: 3D-looking on-screen pages through a whole film; the cards' pop-in animation; a number pop-up; search bars over a
blurred frame; a grid of items plopping in; an opening that shows all its frames, then zooms into the first -> the line: these
techniques hold.

**PI80** (long form) FLAGGED: the like and comment pop-ups missing at the end; a subscribe pop-up 5 s before the spoken ask -> the line:
every long video's last line carries the sign-off in the look's design (the like on "like", the comment on "comments"); a pop-up lands
on its spoken word.

**PI81** FLAGGED: speech bubbles stacked on top of each other -> the line: one at a time.

**PI82** (Shorts) FLAGGED: a split layout: a blurred copy on top, the small clip below, captions and an asset -> the line: too much to
look at; a blurred clip works only as a backdrop behind a pop-in. PASSED: the full-screen layout with centred captions.

**PI83** FLAGGED: a logo fading in over a blurred, whitened copy of the shot before (meant as mist; mist was first mentioned 12 s later),
read as a render bug and a washed-out picture; PASSED as the fix: the logo alone, sharp, dead centre on a dimmed real ground, landing on
its word -> the line: an effect reads only as what the viewer already knows; a picture washed or blurred over its own shot reads as
broken.

**PI84** FLAGGED: emoji standing in as the graphic -> the line: an emoji is a prop inside a real-material piece, never the graphic.

**PI85** FLAGGED: two pictures out of proportion (the house cat bigger than the tiger); a screen whose point was unclear; cluttered,
messy frames -> the line: things at their true relative sizes; every screen's point reads at once; simpler beats busier, and no frame
ever looks messy.

**PI86** FLAGGED: the things the line names greyed and blurred until they couldn't be seen -> the line: what the line names stays
visible.

## Marks: pointing at a thing in a picture
**PI90** FLAGGED: a box drawn round the thing (a 4-px border in the brand colour, the rest dimmed), used for everything -> the line:
a box round a thing looks ugly; the mark is designed (premium, of the picture) and goes to the creator as a still before any video
carries it.

**PI91** FLAGGED: a gentle dim (x0.62) with a wide light and a soft halo outside the thing; PASSED: a strong dim (x0.40) with a tighter
light and a thin rim on the thing's own outline -> the line: the mark dims hard and lights the thing's own outline. The chat's own read
of a strong dim as too dark was wrong, twice.

**PI92** FLAGGED: that rim drawn in the brand colour -> the line: the light alone points at the thing; no brand colour on it.

**PI93** PASSED, of three tries: a 1.2x zoom of the 4K pixels with the rest at ~22% light (over a whole-frame spotlight, and over a
deeper dim) -> the line: the dim keeps the room readable round the lit thing (about a fifth of its light), never near-black; once a
version reads right, stop.

**PI94** PASSED: the lit thing raised to its brightest (its whites unclipped), soft from a 2.4x zoom of a 4K master -> the line: the
exposed thing is bright enough to pop; softness from a needed zoom onto a small thing is accepted.

**PI95** FLAGGED as overlit: a light sized tight to a small thing (it became a glowing disc); a lift that lit the sky round dark things
into a white hot spot; PASSED: the zoomed version with a wide, soft light and no lift -> the line: the light is at least ~0.18 x 0.22 of
the frame; a lift only on a thing that is itself a light; the dim comes in over ~0.4 s.

**PI96** FLAGGED: a small on-screen indicator (a phone's battery icon) lit in the whole frozen frame, too small to see on a phone -> the line: the dark round a
small thing stays, and the picture zooms into it quickly (~0.45 s) from the freeze, cut from a sharp master so the zoom stays crisp; an
indicator that blinks keeps playing under its zoom.

**PI97** FLAGGED: two things named together, only one lit (each mark's own dim switched the other off) -> the line: things named
together light together.

**PI98** FLAGGED: the retired box look back on generated shots, on the wrong object (measured on a frame 0.4 s off the frozen one) ->
the line: the spot is the default mark; a target is measured on the exact frame it marks; a line that says nothing is there gets no mark
at all.

**PI99** FLAGGED: (long form) circle callouts: off-centre, cut off, too tight, misplaced, overused -> the line: no circles; where a
pointer is needed, an arrow, pointing at its subject with a gap at both ends (the tip short of the thing, the tail clear of its word).

**PI100** FLAGGED: marks on things everyone already sees (an object in a hand, a dog in the middle of the frame, a reflection the window
already frames, a detail already zoomed in) -> the line: a mark is for a detail people would miss; none where the zoom already shows it.

**PI101** FLAGGED: marks placed by eye from memory, missing their subjects; a highlight starting inside a letter or ending mid-word; a
highlight on text cropped off-screen; a pointer aimed at the icon next to the thing named -> the line: every mark is checked on its own
frames, on the thing the words name; a highlight starts and ends on the word's edges.

**PI102** FLAGGED: redundant labels and tags (a label over a crossed-out button, corner tags on what the voice already says) -> the
line: no label repeats the voice or the picture.

**PI103** FLAGGED: a creator shown as a text tag -> the line: their profile picture or their channel page.

**PI104** (Shorts) FLAGGED: circles on three counted things, badly placed again; PASSED: the fix, numbers 1, 2, 3 popping over each,
quickly, each with a pop; a word-by-word highlighter sweep with the marker sound on a card -> the line: counting and highlighting happen
on the picture, quickly, each with its sound.

**PI105** FLAGGED: a fast pan and zoom across a skyline toward one far building under construction, so fast it became incoherent -> the
line: to point at one thing in a picture, keep the picture whole and still, dim everything else, and light the thing on its word, with
no move to it; only a thing too small to see gets the quick zoom of PI96.

## Text on screen
**PI110** (long form) FLAGGED: a number said as a statement with no stamp -> the line: a number said as a statement gets the stamp
(the default); phrases and questions never.

**PI111** FLAGGED: stamped statements on screen 0.3-0.5 s before the cut, landing 0.6 s after their words; PASSED in the same piece: a
stamp held 1.6 s -> the line: words on screen stand at least their reading time (1.2 s, +0.03 s per character past 10) before anything
cuts them, and land on their word; a statement at a line's end holds into the next line (the gate's TEXT_SHORT check).

**PI112** FLAGGED: a version leaning on text: half its frames article pages or number cards; artists named in text instead of shown ->
the line: the default is a real picture; text is for a quick, deliberate beat (a big word popping in mid-screen, a short corner note in
a how-to), and text cards are rare.

**PI113** FLAGGED: a view count told as a big-number card over a blurred frame -> the line: show the thing itself (the video, the post,
the page), sharp, with its number lit on it.

**PI114** FLAGGED: text over a busy area (on sunlit foliage); a logo crammed into a corner, nearly cropped; a price layout with uneven sizes
and gaps; a caption squeezed under a picture -> the line: text sits on calm ground, proportionate, with even gaps; sized first, then
placed; where things meet they layer, nothing shrunk to fit.

**PI115** FLAGGED: a title card that looked unprofessional; PASSED: a thin, plain title card chosen over three heavier designs -> the
line: clean type over heavy styling.

**PI116** (long form) FLAGGED: highlights in the Shorts' caption key colour in a long video; a speaker's quote shown plainly, where the
fix asked for was a big speech bubble with its key words in the brand accent colour -> the line: key words in long-form text use the
brand accent; Shorts captions keep their own colour.

**PI117** FLAGGED: a circle added over a detail already zoomed in, under a source clip's own subtitled line; PASSED: the subtitle alone
-> the line: other people's lines are subtitled at the bottom with the speaker's name, and need nothing more.

**PI118** FLAGGED: an on-screen label naming a thing wrongly (a general word for the species the voice named) -> the line: a label says
exactly what the words say.

**PI119** FLAGGED: a numbered list's big number card -> the line: ugly and redundant; a numbered series carries its number small in a
corner.

**PI120** FLAGGED: captions burned with the speech model's spellings of names -> the line: a name on screen is spelled as the script
writes it.

## Shorts
**PI130** FLAGGED: shots held far too long; one clip held ~6 s; the first quarter looking the same throughout; the first 6 s on one
clip -> the line: a new, genuinely different picture every 2-3 s (quick pans or cut-aways where a shot must stay).

**PI131** FLAGGED: two pictures alternating, one shot re-cut with zooms; more cuts made as squeezed millisecond clips -> the line: the
rule is variety, not cutting.

**PI132** FLAGGED: ~1 s of music before the voice; a dead tail after the last line; a frozen image padding the end -> the line: the
voice runs from frame 0 to the last line.

**PI133** FLAGGED: the opening flash missing; its sound swapped for a loud metal hit; PASSED: the flash with its sound -> the line:
every Short opens with the house flash and its sound on frame 0.

**PI134** FLAGGED: 9:16 crops that miss the subject (framed on a door, a dull pillar, an elevator showing nothing, a dim room; the
subject cut off); the key part of a screenshot never in frame -> the line: the most important part is centred, vertically too; a boring
frame is fatal in a Short.

**PI135** FLAGGED: a black bar at the bottom from the zoom framing -> the line: the base zoom always fills the whole 9:16 frame; no
bars, no blur bands.

**PI136** FLAGGED: pans with no job; a pan landing off centre; a camera framing nothing until the freeze -> the line: a pan has a job,
starts on its subject and lands centred.

**PI137** FLAGGED: a Short told on pages as the hero (posts, emails, headlines: 16 of 23 shots, two thirds of its time) with credit
labels on its pictures; PASSED, as the level for every Short: the same voice rebuilt with every page replaced by act-outs (a parcel flying
past the people it was meant for, a door swinging open on them, a phone held out with the caller's name, the camera diving into a screen that
switches off like an old TV, a gavel on "lawyers") -> the line: in a Short no shot's hero is text; every line is acted out by footage, a
frame or our own animation, and the captions carry the words. Text that merely sits inside a frame is fine. No credits on screen: an
attribution goes in the description.

**PI138** PASSED: a story told with built paper props on a real paper texture, the receipts lit as said, the real people's own photos;
the actual object a story is about (its cover, its photo) shown as the evidence -> the line: built act-outs on real textures and the
real people and objects carry a story Short.

**PI139** PASSED: a hook of three pairs of pictures split above and below the captions, ~0.5 s each -> the line: a fast paired hook
works.

**PI140** FLAGGED: captions whose size changed with the word count; captions not 1:1 with the voice; commas and periods shown; a name
split across two captions; a last caption ending in a dash; captions off-centre in a split layout -> the line: captions are full words,
1:1 with the voice, one fixed size, no punctuation but "?", a name kept together, dead centre.

**PI141** FLAGGED: captions in another colour than the chosen caption colours -> the line: the captions stay in the look's caption
colours on every Short.

**PI142** FLAGGED: text hidden behind the phone's status bar (three uploads lost to it) -> the line: nothing in the status bar's zone;
text sits mid-screen, or at the top well below the status bar.

**PI143** FLAGGED: a static label above the captions (two texts at once); labels parked at the top; hero text and pop-ins sitting higher
than the caption line; an animated label -> the line: one text at a time; a word that shows the thing (a logo, a hero word) takes the
