# Sound: the casebook

Every verdict of the creator on the effects, the music and the mix goes here. The voice itself (takes, accent, pauses) is `VOICE.md`.
Read in every sound pass. Method and entry format: `README.md`. The seed lines below are the starting taste; the creator's cases join
them under each heading and replace any line they contradict.

## Effects: a sound for a thing seen
**SO1** FLAGGED: a whoosh with no clip change; a pop with nothing on screen; sounds left behind after a re-edit (a pencil sound where
the mark had been removed) -> the line: every effect has something visible to attach to; after every edit, each effect is checked
against its event again.

**SO2** FLAGGED: effects a little early or late (a bass drop before its word, a drop before its big text); a marker sound 0.9 s off its
light, which the gate had flagged and the chat had allowed from what the code was meant to do instead of measuring -> the line: a sound
plays on its event's first frame; a gate flag on sync is measured, never allowed by reasoning.

**SO3** FLAGGED as out of sync, where the measurement found every sound on its frame; PASSED: the same cue sheet as an A/B on a fresh
render -> the line: when the measure says in sync and the ear said not, an A/B on a fresh render settles it in one round instead of a
guessed fix.

**SO4** FLAGGED: an effect on every item of a run of graphics, over and over across a minute, until it seemed to play all the time;
PASSED: a pop per date as three dates come into one timeline, plus a marker on its span, inside two seconds -> the line: one effect per
graphic beat, never one per item across a stretch (about four a minute, never more than three in five seconds there); a short burst on
one graphic's own events is fine. It is the repetition across a stretch that tires the ear.

**SO5** FLAGGED: the count-up sound under numbers that simply land, used so much it hurt; its whole 3.4 s file under a 1.3 s fill -> the
line: the count-up only under digits visibly rolling, and a sound standing for an action with a length ends with it.

**SO6** FLAGGED: a clock tick under "it takes longer" -> the line: no clock tick, ever, whatever the picture.

**SO7** FLAGGED: odd choices: a bomb-countdown tick, a xylophone-like bed, a piercing rewind, a random noise on a line, a jail sound
that didn't fit, a synthesized censor hit, a loud metal hit in place of the opening flash's sound -> the line: plain, well-known, punchy
hits; the house set first.

**SO8** FLAGGED: a draft with no effects; a graphics Short with almost none; effects and pings too quiet; too little variety -> the
line: enough effects, clearly audible but never overpowering, and varied.

**SO9** FLAGGED: a rewind sound with no rewind on screen -> the line: a sound matches what is seen.

**SO10** FLAGGED: a bass drop cut off by a scene change -> the line: a boom is never cut short.

**SO11** PASSED: a bass drop with an instant zoom on the hook's key word; drops synced to the key reveals; whooshes on the clip changes
-> the line: the bass drop belongs to a key word or a reveal, together with its big text or zoom.

**SO12** FLAGGED: a mark landing with the wrong house sound; a highlight's sound that didn't fit; PASSED: the marker sound chosen for
highlights -> the line: each kind of event keeps its own house sound (the pop with a mark, the marker with a highlight, the camera
shutter with a screenshot landing).

**SO13** FLAGGED: a risky word in our narration covered by a synthesized hit; a distortion laid over a swear inside a source clip;
PASSED: a bass-boost hit at half volume -> the line: in our narration a risky word is avoided, or covered with a bass-boost hit, never a
beep or a distortion; a source clip's own swearing plays as recorded.

**SO14** FLAGGED: the chat recreating a meme's audio itself -> the line: the real meme audio, from its source.

**SO15** (Shorts) FLAGGED: a speaker's own pauses kept inside a bite (7 s with long gaps) -> the line: other people's speech is
tightened like ours, its pauses cut to ~0.07 s at the speech model's word edges before it is laid in; there is never a moment with no
talking.

**SO16** FLAGGED: the chat asking the creator to pick the music or the effects -> the line: the chat picks them, from its study and the
house set.

## Music
**SO20** (long form) FLAGGED: a score that overcooked, until the creator stopped watching: a loud, sudden track entrance; a hype beat
and a beat drop under a serious stretch; a hype track cutting straight into a sad piano; an upbeat, happy track under the mid-video ask
breaking a mysterious mood; music suddenly twice as loud, fighting the voice; PASSED: the score of the film's first part, and after the
loud start -> the line: long-form music is quiet, simple ambience at a steady level that fits the mood and isn't noticed; switches are
smooth; voice and music complement, never compete.

**SO21** FLAGGED: two beds at different tempos at once; a higher layer stacked on a sub-bass opening (the sub-bass alone passed);
overlapping, badly cut music at a transition; a high chirp inside a bed; a glitch inside a track -> the line: one bed at a time, clean
joins, a clean track.

**SO22** FLAGGED: music fading out and staying quiet too long; a music pause badly timed; a pause that ended too early -> the line:
music runs continuously at a steady level; a short, deliberate mute on one line (a punchline) is fine when it stops exactly at the line
and leaves no dead silence; a long drop in level is not.

**SO23** FLAGGED: the same three beds kept across a long film -> the line: scored scene by scene; mood switches between sections are
good for variety when they are smooth.

**SO24** FLAGGED: a song entrance at the title that didn't match its moment -> the line: a track enters on its moment.

**SO25** FLAGGED: a clip's own music stripped so ours could sit under it; voices in a clip run through stem separation (they sounded
wrong) -> the line: a source clip's audio plays as it is.

**SO26** (Shorts) PASSED: a track under the narration, stopped dead under a speaker's bite; a dark suspense track ~17 dB under the
voice, stopped under both bites, with markers on every lit line and pops on every name -> the line: a Short's music sits ~17-18 dB under
the voice with one effect per visible event, and stops dead under a bite.

**SO27** (Shorts) FLAGGED: no music in a draft; a bed too quiet on a phone; a bed a touch too loud -> the line: audible under the voice,
judged on a phone.

**SO28** (Shorts) FLAGGED: proposed music options that were dull library beds (all but one rejected) -> the line: a strong track per
Short that fits its tone (for a mystery: engaging, mysterious, dramatic).

## The mix
**SO30** FLAGGED: a final mix far too loud (the creator had to turn the system volume from ~50 down to 20); PASSED: the quietest of
three loudness versions -> the line: a mix goes out at its measured loudness target, comfortable at a normal volume.
