# Coherence: the casebook (does it make sense?)

Every verdict of the creator on whether a line, the picture under a line, or a clip in its place makes sense goes here: the ones they
pass as much as the ones they flag, so the line between coherent, fine and too incoherent can be seen and predicted. The coherency
read's packet carries this file whole: every reader calibrates on it before it flags anything, and the chat reads it before judging a
flag. Method and entry format: `README.md`. The seed lines below are the starting taste; the creator's cases join them under each
heading and replace any line they contradict.

## Where the line falls
**CO1** FLAGGED and PASSED, across one review of a dozen flagged spots: every spot where a viewer could be left guessing (who an
unintroduced name is, which of two places "there" means) was wanted fixed; every real picture that showed the named thing, even
loosely, was passed -> the line: clarity for the viewer weighs more than it seems; a real picture of the named thing stands. Flag what
leaves a viewer guessing; pass what shows the named thing, even loosely.

## The picture under the words
**CO2** FLAGGED: footage picked by a keyword, "related" to the line but not what it says (a person on the phone under "talk to the
people around you", a clip of the wrong stroke under a line about switching from crawl to backstroke); a weak picture under a key phrase while the
footage on disk held better -> the line: the picture shows exactly what the words say, the moment they say it; "related" fails. Every
option for a line is weighed, never the first match.

**CO3** FLAGGED: a built graphic under a line about something the footage shows (two climbers roped together on a ridge, told as a graphic)
-> the line: a line about something the footage shows gets that footage, not a graphic standing for it.

**CO4** FLAGGED: two faces in circles under "they walk on stage together", while the band's own concert film has one of them waving
the other on -> the line: the obvious literal moment exists, and missing it is the error. The masters are searched for the literal
moment first, before anything is built; the creator knows the footage.

**CO5** FLAGGED: a line naming one act ("you slice it lengthwise, not across") over a clip of the other act, while footage of the named
act exists -> the line: when the words name an action and real footage of it exists, the picture is that action; a picture of a
different or opposite act is wrong.

**CO6** PASSED: the only real example of a feature in the primary source's material (a message arriving on a phone screen) under a line
whose exact words the message on screen does not match -> the line: the picture shows the mechanism the words name; the only real
example of a thing outweighs a closer fit. The words may bend toward the only clip; the clip never goes for the words.

**CO7** PASSED: an older version's picture of the exact thing named (the old model's feature), under "it hasn't shown up in the new one
yet" -> the line: another source's picture of the named thing is fine; how to mark it as the other source's (its tag, a fade to grey on
"shown up") is the chat's call as the editor.

**CO8** FLAGGED: footage from another product or source under a line about the subject, with a tiny credit or none -> the line: footage
from another source carries that source's tag, big enough to read.

**CO9** PASSED: one of the main people shown in the situation the words name (on crutches), though the event in the words happens to a
stranger -> the line: a main person in the named situation is fine; a tight crop that looks ugly is worse than a loose fit that looks
good.

**CO10** FLAGGED: one of the main people standing in for a stranger the words describe in action, while the clip of the tool the line is
about exists (a line about a doorbell camera and what visitors do when it chimes) -> the line: a line about a tool or a feature plays
that tool's own clip; the main people never stand in for a stranger the words describe.

**CO11** FLAGGED: a small, soft still of a camera's settings menu blown up ~3x, while normal-size clips of that menu in use exist -> the line: a feature is
shown as its real footage in use, at its normal size, even if not every item named appears in it; never a blown-up still when the clip
exists.

**CO12** FLAGGED: nothing the words name on screen (a line about a chef grabbing a blowtorch, over a scene with neither); PASSED as the
fix: the chef on screen, then a real photo of the blowtorch landing on its word -> the line: when nothing the words name is on screen, the
picture is wrong; a real prop landing on its word is an accepted way to show an object no footage holds.

**CO13** FLAGGED (the chat's pick between two pictures for "the ranger warns you first", where no clip of the warning exists): a calm
picture of strangers posing at the trailhead, picked over a picture of a ranger standing behind one of the main people, who looks ready
to bolt -> the line: the picture that carries the moment's feeling (tension, a main person in it) beats a calm picture of strangers, even as
a repeat. When choosing between pictures: the main people and the tension first.

**CO14** FLAGGED: the same on-screen message bubble reused under a line about a different message -> the line: when no footage shows the
exact thing, a smart editor builds it: a real-looking object (a phone with that very message arriving) acting out exactly what the words
say.

**CO15** FLAGGED: a cut-in that changed what a word refers to (a shop scene under "that", so the line read as being about the shop);
PASSED as the fix: the primary source's own shot of the claim itself; PASSED later: a visual joke selling the words (a pixel mosaic
tracked frame by frame over a body for "with no clothes on at all", the clothes faint through it) -> the line: the picture under a claim
shows the claim itself. A playful visual joke that sells the words is welcome when it is done precisely (tracked, never sliding off),
and only then; an idea that only works if done well goes to the creator as a sample before it ships, with the plain clip kept as the
fallback.

**CO16** FLAGGED: things named with no picture of their own (a person, a song, a brand, a city named as plain text or not shown at all;
a line naming who does something over a clip of someone else) -> the line: everything named gets its own visual the instant it is named:
the person's photo, the brand's logo, the song's cover, the city itself; a line naming a person shows that person.

**CO17** FLAGGED: a spoken list ("the market, the old fort, the night ferry, the hot springs, diving") over one frame shown the longest;
three knife cuts said, shown as one clip with a label; a landmark named over the picture of the one before it -> the line: every item of a list
gets its own picture on its word, with the item as the subject; a label is never a substitute for footage; when the source's own screen
shows the words (its own caption naming the place), that clip is the picture.

**CO18** FLAGGED: a picture that is not what people know the word by (a rare hairstyle under "short hair", for a person who wears a buzz
cut nearly all the time) -> the line: a word gets the picture people know it by.

**CO19** FLAGGED: a small view lifted and enlarged from a dull moment, while the footage holds a moment where the same thing shows
clearly -> the line: the clearest moment of the thing beats a lifted crop; the masters are searched for the moment the words describe
best.

**CO20** FLAGGED: a claim about an on-screen indicator ("the sensor sees nobody in the room") shown on a calm, empty moment; a claim with no
visual proof on screen at all -> the line: a claim is shown on the moment that proves it: the cause and the indicator both in the
picture (people right there in the shot, none on the sensor's screen). An indicator that blinks keeps playing under its zoom.

**CO21** FLAGGED: a trained dog's skill named, shown as a rating bar -> the line: an ability is shown in use.

**CO22** FLAGGED: a general cooking shot under "if you burn the garlic", while the same recipe video holds that exact moment (the garlic
going black in the pan); an act shown by people only walking toward it -> the line: the exact moment the words name, from the same
sequence, beats a general one; the act itself, never the walk to it.

**CO23** FLAGGED: a number that a well-known clip seems to contradict ("0 bags", while a known clip shows a rider carrying one) -> the
line: the number is shown with that clip and the words that reconcile it ("none in the bike's storage, two on the riders").

**CO24** FLAGGED: a famous TV moment described in words instead of played -> the line: the thing talked about is played or shown itself;
short clips of shows, films and other creators are normal content.

**CO25** (Shorts) PASSED: a background from outside the niche when the words are about something else; a background of any genre that is
visually appealing -> the line: a background fits its words and looks good; it need not come from the niche.

## The words making sense
**CO30** FLAGGED: an interview answer ("it's all of it") placed right after a line about something else -> the line: an answer carries
its own question; the viewer must have heard what it answers.

**CO31** FLAGGED: the narration quoting a person while that person talks on screen, then their voice running on under an unrelated graph
-> the line: a real person speaking is shown speaking; the narration never talks over them, and their voice never sits under an
unrelated graphic.

**CO32** FLAGGED: a clause whose who or what the viewer was never given ("and the company's guy showing it had never heard that line
before") -> the line: a clause with no setup is cut (most viewers would be confused); the fact it carries stays.

**CO33** FLAGGED: an ambiguous "there" after two places had been named -> the line: fixed in the picture (the meant place on screen on
its word) when the voice is already locked; otherwise fixed in the words.

**CO34** FLAGGED: a name the viewer may not know (an artist, a band) used with no introduction -> the line: introduced, on screen at
least.

**CO35** PASSED: a speaker's own bite adding a detail the line before it didn't say (a year's gap) -> the line: a bite that adds a
detail reads as the payoff, not a contradiction.

**CO36** PASSED: the hook's promise ("and they got the last word") paid at the end by the thing's own words -> the line: the payoff the
hook names, in the thing's own words.

**CO37** FLAGGED: words on screen that make no sense on their own -> the line: everything on screen makes sense read alone.

**CO38** FLAGGED: a speaker's clip cut to a span that missed the sentence that makes the point, too short to carry its context; PASSED:
speaker clips played long enough, with their context -> the line: a bite is the span that says the thing, long enough to be understood
on its own.
